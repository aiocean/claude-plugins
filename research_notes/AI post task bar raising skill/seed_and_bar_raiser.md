## KQ1. What exactly do the seed skill and its sibling files say, and how do the pieces fit together?

### Takeaway
The seed is a real, fetchable skill (a 270-line SKILL.md) plus a 141-line reviewer persona, a 356-line operating contract (AGENTS.md), and a procedural-memory skill. Together they form a loop: *review diff against explicit criteria -> anchored, severity-ranked findings -> verdict (APPROVED / APPROVED WITH NOTES / NOT APPROVED / INCOMPLETE) -> findings carry lifecycle states across re-reviews -> recurring findings are distilled into at most 12 procedural rules that are injected into the next build*. The anti-rationalization machinery is concentrated in the persona file ("golden rule", "no anchor, no entry", "do not rubber-stamp") rather than in the SKILL.md rationalizations table, which is generic.

**Implementation-memory (the "improve after a task" link)**

- Hard limits: max 12 active rules, max 120 words per rule, max 2 candidate learnings per review, "Rewrite the whole file when updating; never append a chronological build log" ([#L62-L70](https://github.com/robisson/build-like-amazon-agent-skills/blob/1d2e59d5f05ee8c08a64678e9198b229db694394/skills/implementation-memory/SKILL.md#L62-L70)). HIGH.
- Rule schema: ID, Tags, Phase, File patterns, Applies when, Rule, Avoid, Evidence, Impact, Confidence (Proven/Established/New), Hit count, Prevented, Created, Last used, Builds on ([#L72-L92](https://github.com/robisson/build-like-amazon-agent-skills/blob/1d2e59d5f05ee8c08a64678e9198b229db694394/skills/implementation-memory/SKILL.md#L72-L92)). "`Prevented` is an evidence counter, not a confidence signal. Increment it only when something concrete stopped ... Applying a rule, or believing it helped, is not evidence" (#L96). HIGH.
- Capture trigger and sequence: after the verdict is PASSED WITH FIXES NEEDED, the agent "generate[s] a structured self-reflection (2-3 sentences): 'What went wrong? Why? What would have prevented it?'", extracts up to 2 candidates from the reflection (not the raw findings), shows them to the user as Accept / Reject / Edit ([#L119-L148](https://github.com/robisson/build-like-amazon-agent-skills/blob/1d2e59d5f05ee8c08a64678e9198b229db694394/skills/implementation-memory/SKILL.md#L119-L148); #L166). HIGH.
- Admission: a candidate must pass at least 2 of 5 checks (avoids a likely future bug; avoids repeated review rework; improves tests/security/ops/rollout; applies beyond one feature; "Corrects a recurring agent tendency") ([#L182-L190](https://github.com/robisson/build-like-amazon-agent-skills/blob/1d2e59d5f05ee8c08a64678e9198b229db694394/skills/implementation-memory/SKILL.md#L182-L190)). HIGH.
- Escalation: "A defect that recurs even though a memory rule already covers it means memory is the wrong remedy: promote it to a declared project pattern or a mechanism via `skills/mechanism-creation/SKILL.md`, and do not add a second memory rule" ([#L192](https://github.com/robisson/build-like-amazon-agent-skills/blob/1d2e59d5f05ee8c08a64678e9198b229db694394/skills/implementation-memory/SKILL.md#L192)). HIGH.

## KQ2. What is Amazon's Bar Raiser role in hiring and in other uses?

## KQ3. Which properties of the Bar Raiser mechanism drive quality; which can an AI agent reproduce and which cannot?

### Takeaway
The properties the sources actually emphasize are: independence from the requisition (no stake), an explicit and consistent standard ("better than 50% of peers" + Leadership Principles), authority that can stop the hire (latent veto), trained judgment via apprenticeship and debrief practice, and a data-driven debrief. An agent can reproduce the explicit standard, the written rationale, evidence anchoring, and a stop/no-go verdict; it can approximate independence only structurally (fresh context, different instructions, read-only role, ideally a different model); it cannot reproduce organizational authority, genuinely uncorrelated judgment from the same model, or human calibration-by-apprenticeship. No source I found measures which Bar Raiser property causes the quality gain; that attribution is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Independence is the property every source names first: "not the hiring manager or a recruiter" ([Carr](https://workingbackwards.com/blog/the-history-and-impact-of-amazons-bar-raiser-hiring-program/)); "typically sit on another team and serve as an objective third party" ([AWS careers](https://aws.amazon.com/careers/life-at-aws-amazons-bar-raiser-program-hiring-for-long-term-growth-and-innovation)); "not for a specific team or role" ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/hire-power-how-amazonians-raise-the-bar-with-every-interview)). HIGH.

HIGH.
- Authority: veto per Carr/book (see KQ2 conflict); the stated purpose is to protect standards from "the 'here and now' demands of the business" (Fellow snippet, LOW-MEDIUM). Carr: the veto's power is latent: "never saw it used directly". HIGH for the quote.
- Training and calibration: 3 months to 1 year ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-bar-raiser)); "up to 10 hours a week", shadowing and running debriefs ([Carr](https://workingbackwards.com/blog/the-history-and-impact-of-amazons-bar-raiser-hiring-program/)). MEDIUM-LOW.
- Written rationale / data-driven debrief: "ensure data-driven conversations" ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-bar-raiser)). HIGH that Amazon says this; how rationale is recorded is not described in the fetched pages.
- Evidence on whether the program raises outcomes: Carr's claim is "It became a core part of how we scaled while protecting what made us successful" ([Carr](https://workingbackwards.com/blog/the-history-and-impact-of-amazons-bar-raiser-hiring-program/)) - an insider assertion, not data. A search for research on Bar Raiser calibration/consistency found no peer-reviewed or Amazon-published reliability study (only vendor blogs and anecdotes such as one Glassdoor review of a Bar Raiser asking irrelevant granular questions that flipped a panel). HIGH that I found none; the anecdote is LOW.
- Amazon itself removed Bar Raisers from some entry-level loops during volume growth and reinstated them (Fortune, snippet only, MEDIUM) - a signal that the independent role is costly and tends to erode under throughput pressure.

The skill proper provides only "read your own code as if seeing it for the first time" (#L47), which a model cannot do because the authoring context is still in its window.
2. **"Ship It" is a social commitment ("Your name is on it", tenet 1, #L264) with no social cost for an agent.** The deterrence that makes the human Bar Raiser work (authors write to the bar because a veto exists) is absent when author = gate.
3. **The reviewer reads its own justifications.** Context from the build (why a shortcut was taken, what was "known to be fine") leaks into judgment, so `[QUESTION]`/`INCOMPLETE` are unlikely to be raised on code the agent "remembers" understanding. The seed's INCOMPLETE rule ("every line you did not understand must be declared") relies on honest self-assessment of understanding, which is the thing a self-reviewing agent is least able to assess.
4. **Finding generation vs finding admission are the same mind.** "No anchor, no entry" and the golden rule work as filters against false positives, but a self-reviewer biased toward "done" will apply the discard filter asymmetrically (discard as "preference" or "no evidence" the findings that would force rework). The lifecycle's `FIXED` requires a re-read, but the re-reader is the fixer (build.md#L320 forbids exactly this).
5. **`NOT-REPRODUCIBLE` and `ACCEPTED-WITH-RISK` become escape hatches.** The latter requires "the human who owns the decision" to set it ([SKILL.md#L197](https://github.com/robisson/build-like-amazon-agent-skills/blob/1d2e59d5f05ee8c08a64678e9198b229db694394/skills/code-review-bar-raising/SKILL.md#L197)) - a good guard - but `NOT-REPRODUCIBLE` may be set by "the re-review" alone (#L198), i.e., by the agent.
6. **Memory capture amplifies self-assessment errors.** Candidate learnings come from the agent's own "self-reflection" (implementation-memory#L166), with a human Accept/Reject/Edit gate as the only external check. If no human is present (autonomous run) the gate vanishes; the skill's flow says candidates are "presented to the user", so autonomous use needs a defined fallback, which the seed does not give for non-interactive runs. (The repo's AGENTS.md has an "autonomous /build" mode, #L161-L171, but it does not address memory capture without a human.) HIGH that the fallback is absent in the files I read.
7. **Threshold gaming.** Fixed counts (12 rules, 2 candidates, 3 rounds) bound cost, but a single agent can satisfy "N checks done" without a real check; the seed's counter on `Prevented` ("Applying a rule, or believing it helped, is not evidence", implementation-memory#L96) is a good partial defense because it demands a concrete stopper (validator, failing test, cited review).

### Inferences
- Reusable as-is: verdict vocabulary (APPROVED / WITH NOTES / NOT APPROVED / INCOMPLETE), finding shape, the golden rule, declared-criteria precedence, evidence-gated status transitions, capped procedural memory with an evidence counter and "memory is the wrong remedy -> mechanism" escape. (MEDIUM.)
- Needs replacement for a generic post-task improver: the five code dimensions (task-type-specific criteria must come from the task itself or the project), the human calibration items, the persona-loading across files, and the many alias tables. (MEDIUM.)
- A seed-compatible way to restore independence without a second human: fresh sub-agent with artifacts only (the seed's own `/build` approach), read-only reviewer, and preferably deterministic checks (tests, linters, type check) whose outcomes the reviewer cannot argue with; the seed's `tools/bla-check` hints at this direction but I did not read it. (MEDIUM, design suggestion outside my facet's non-goals; flagged for the synthesis.)
