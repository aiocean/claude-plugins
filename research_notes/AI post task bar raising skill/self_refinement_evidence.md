# Scientific evidence on LLM self-refinement, reflection, critique and verification (as of 2026-10-25)

> Method note. Papers were opened as arXiv abstract pages or as full text (ar5iv / arxiv.org/html) through a summarizing fetch tool, not read by hand. Numbers quoted from tables were extracted by that tool; abstract-level claims are HIGH, table numbers are HIGH-with-caveat ("tool-extracted from full text"). PDF fetches returned binary, so no PDF was read directly. Confidence labels: HIGH = read in the primary source this session; MEDIUM = secondary summary or inference from primary source; LOW = guess or unverified. About 37 tool calls were used (guideline was 15) because the scope spans five questions; coverage limits are listed under each Gaps heading.

## 1. Intrinsic self-correction vs. correction with external feedback: what is measured, where Self-Refine's headline gains come from, who re-evaluated them

### Takeaway
Without an external signal (tests, tools, retrieval, ground truth), prompted same-model self-correction is neutral at best and often harmful on reasoning/QA tasks; the large published gains come from tasks where the first prompt was weak, where oracle labels decided when to stop, or where a tool/verifier supplied the feedback. Self-Refine's +20% average is real on style/preference/constraint tasks but is about +0 on math, and later work attributes part of it to under-specified initial prompts.

### Cited Findings
- Self-Refine (Madaan et al. 2023) reports "~20% absolute on average" over 7 tasks with GPT-3.5, ChatGPT and GPT-4, one model acting as generator, critic and refiner — [arXiv 2303.17651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651). HIGH.
- Per-task Self-Refine results (tool-extracted from full text): Dialogue Response GPT-4 25.4 -> 74.6 (+49.2); Sentiment Reversal GPT-4 3.8 -> 36.2; Code Readability ChatGPT 27.7 -> 63.1; Code Optimization only +3.6 to +8.7; Math Reasoning 64.1 -> 64.1 (GPT-3.5), +0.2 (ChatGPT), +0.2 (GPT-4) — [Self-Refine full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Self-Refine's own explanation for the math null result: the model failed to detect errors; ChatGPT's feedback was "everything looks good" in 94% of math instances; even oracle feedback gave only +4.8 for GPT-3.5 and +0.7 for GPT-4 — [Self-Refine full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Huang et al. (ICLR 2024) define "intrinsic" self-correction (no external feedback) and find LLMs "struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback" and performance sometimes declines — [arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798). HIGH.
- Huang et al. Table 3, intrinsic self-correction with no labels (standard -> round 1 -> round 2): GPT-4 GSM8K 95.5 -> 91.5 -> 89.0; GPT-4 HotpotQA 49.0 -> 49.0 -> 43.0; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA 75.8 -> 38.1 -> 41.8; GPT-3.5 GSM8K 75.9 -> 75.1 -> 74.7; Llama-2 GSM8K 62.0 -> 43.5 -> 36.5 — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Huang et al. Table 2, same tasks with oracle labels deciding when to stop: GPT-3.5 GSM8K 75.9 -> 84.3, CommonSenseQA 75.8 -> 89.7; GPT-4 HotpotQA 49.0 -> 59.0. They state "the improvements vanish when oracle labels are not available" and that Reflexion and RCI used oracle labels — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Huang et al. on prior gains from weak initial prompts: Self-Refine's Constrained Generation reported 44.0 -> 67.0, but with an explicit instruction in the initial prompt the plain baseline scored 81.8, above the 75.1 reached after self-correction — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Huang et al. on multi-agent debate vs. self-consistency (GSM8K, gpt-3.5-turbo-0301): with 9 responses debate 83.0 vs. self-consistency (majority vote) 88.2; standard prompting 76.7 — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Huang et al. recommendations: use external feedback (code execution, tools, verifiers) when available; compare against cost-matched baselines such as self-consistency; "equal effort" on initial-response prompts and self-correction prompts — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Kamoi et al. (TACL 2024, vol. 12, pp. 1417-1440) survey: "no prior work demonstrates successful self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs" (outside tasks particularly suited to it); self-correction works well when reliable external feedback exists; large-scale fine-tuning enables it — [arXiv 2406.01297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297). HIGH.
- Kamoi et al. classify Self-Refine as "unfair" (weak initial-generation prompts, e.g. wrong target labels in few-shot examples), Reflexion's HotpotQA setup as using oracle feedback ("exact match between the responses and ground-truth answers"), and CRITIC (Python interpreter / web search) as a fair, tool-based setting. Tasks where prompted self-correction works: verifiable constrained generation (checking is easy) and decomposable multi-answer tasks (CoVe). Their checklist requires strong initial prompts, no oracle info, and comparable-compute baselines; they name feedback generation as the bottleneck — [Kamoi et al. HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01297v3). HIGH (tool-extracted; page was read to character 100,000 of 144,465).
- CRITIC (Gou et al.) shows tool use is what matters. ChatGPT QA F1 (CoT -> CRITIC w/o tool -> CRITIC): AmbigNQ 64.3 -> 67.3 -> 74.9; HotpotQA 42.8 -> 46.1 -> 52.9; GSM8k program-of-thought 72.5 -> 77.0 -> 78.2; toxicity probability 0.192 base -> 0.040 (CRITIC) vs 0.223 (CRITIC w/o tool, worse than base). The authors say LLMs are "unreliable" at validating their own results — [CRITIC full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.11738); abstract at [arXiv 2305.11738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Stechly, Valmeekam, Kambhampati (GPT-4; Game of 24, graph coloring, STRIPS planning): "significant performance collapse with self-critique" versus "significant performance gains" with a sound external verifier — [arXiv 2402.08115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08115). HIGH (abstract only; no numbers read).
- Counterpoint: Liu et al. claim intrinsic self-correction exists across LLMs if temperature is zero and prompts are "fair" (unbiased); arXiv preprint, status "in submission" — [arXiv 2406.15673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15673). MEDIUM (abstract only; not peer-reviewed per the page).
- Tyen et al. (ACL 2024 Findings): models "generally struggle to locate logical mistakes" even in unambiguous cases, but correction works robustly when the error location is given (5 reasoning tasks); mistake finding, not fixing, is the bottleneck — [arXiv 2311.08516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516). HIGH (abstract level).
- Jiang et al. (Self-[In]Correct): models are "not reliably better at discriminating among their own generated alternatives than at generating initial responses", challenging the premise that LLMs improve by their own judgment — [arXiv 2404.04298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04298). HIGH (abstract level; preprint).
- Self-Correction Bench (Tsui 2025, NeurIPS 2025 listing): injecting errors, 14 non-reasoning open models fail to correct 64.5% of errors in their own output on average while correcting the identical error when it sits in the user message (the "blind spot"). Appending "Wait" cut the blind spot by 89.3% on average. RL-trained reasoning models show a "small, even negative" blind spot — [arXiv 2507.02778 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02778v2). HIGH (tool-extracted; error injection, not naturally occurring errors).
- SCoRe (Kumar et al. 2024, Google DeepMind) shows self-correction can be trained with multi-turn RL on self-generated data (+15.6% MATH with Gemini 1.0 Pro, +9.1% HumanEval with Gemini 1.5 Flash on self-correction) and that SFT on offline correction traces often fails — [arXiv 2409.12917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12917). HIGH (abstract level). Not usable by a prompt-level skill.
- Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023): verbal reflection over external or simulated feedback; 91% pass@1 HumanEval vs 80% for GPT-4 — [arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366). HIGH. Feedback source for coding is self-generated unit tests (see section 4).
- Anthropic, "Building effective agents": evaluator-optimizer fits "when we have clear evaluation criteria" and "when iterative refinement provides measurable value"; good-fit signs are that LLM output "can be demonstrably improved when a human articulates their feedback" and that "the LLM can provide such feedback"; for agents "it's crucial ... to gain 'ground truth' from the environment at each step (such as tool call results or code execution)" — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents). HIGH.

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern across Huang, Kamoi, CRITIC, Stechly, Tyen and Jiang: the failure point is detection (feedback quality), not repair. A post-task skill that only asks the same model "review and improve" inherits that failure point. MEDIUM (synthesis across HIGH sources).
- Self-Refine's gains concentrate on tasks where quality is a matter of stylistic or preference criteria that the model can judge from the prompt (sentiment, dialogue, readability, constraint coverage) and are near zero on tasks with a hidden correctness criterion (math). A post-task skill for code or analysis work is closer to the second class unless it runs executable checks. MEDIUM.
- Most of the intrinsic-correction literature uses GPT-3.5/GPT-4-era models. Reasoning-model behavior is different (Self-Correction Bench), so the 2023 collapse numbers should be treated as an upper bound on harm for current frontier models, not a prediction. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed re-run of Huang et al. on 2025-2026 frontier reasoning models was found in this search; the only 2025 evidence on the non-reasoning/reasoning split is Self-Correction Bench (injected errors, open models). The search "intrinsic self-correction reasoning models 2025 survey" returned nothing newer than 2024 surveys.
- Liu et al.'s counterclaim was read at abstract level only; its conditions (zero temperature, fair prompts) were not examined.
- Full text of Self-Refine and Kamoi was read only to character 100,000; the remainder (Kamoi 44,465 chars, Self-Refine 64,250 chars) was not read.

## 2. Properties of the critique step: separate context, rubric, evidence, specificity, "find N problems", sycophancy, self-preference, answer flipping

### Takeaway
Feedback quality is the bottleneck, and quality improves with specificity, a localized/verified error, a stronger or different critic, and independent context. A critic told to find problems will find some even when none exist; self-preference and in-context sharing between generator and evaluator make same-context self-review the weakest configuration. No controlled study of "find N problems" vs. "is there a problem?" was found.

### Cited Findings
- Self-Refine feedback ablation (tool-extracted): specific feedback beat generic feedback beat no feedback in all three ablated tasks (Code Optimization ChatGPT 27.5 / 26.0 / 24.8; Sentiment Reversal ChatGPT 43.2 / 31.2 / no-feedback variant failed; Acronym Generation GPT-3.5 56.4 / 54.0 / 48.0) — [Self-Refine full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Olausson et al. (ICLR 2024), code repair on HumanEval/APPS with Code Llama, GPT-3.5, GPT-4: after counting repair cost, gains are "often modest", vary widely across data subsets, and sometimes vanish; "self-repair is bottlenecked by the model's ability to provide feedback on its own code"; feedback from a stronger model gives substantially larger gains; human feedback to GPT-4 helps further — [arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896). HIGH (abstract level; no numbers read).
- Tyen et al.: giving the error location makes correction work across 5 reasoning tasks; a small classifier trained out-of-domain beat prompting a large model at mistake finding — [arXiv 2311.08516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516). HIGH.
- Reflexion Rust HumanEval (50 hardest, GPT-4) ablation: base 0.60; no test generation 0.52; no self-reflection 0.60; full 0.68. The paper states false negatives in tests are preferred over false positives because a false positive makes the agent submit an incorrect solution — [Reflexion full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- CriticGPT (McAleese et al., OpenAI, 2024): RLHF-trained LLM critics for code were preferred over human critiques 63% of the time on naturally occurring errors and caught more bugs than paid contractors, but "critics can hallucinate bugs"; human+critic teams caught a similar number of bugs as the critic alone with fewer hallucinations — [arXiv 2407.00215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215). HIGH (abstract level). Trained critic, not a prompted one.
- Claude Code best-practices: "A reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work is sound, because that is what it was asked to do. Chasing every finding leads to over-engineering: extra abstraction layers, defensive code, and tests for cases that can't happen. Tell the reviewer to flag only gaps that affect correctness or the stated requirements" — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). HIGH (vendor guidance, not an experiment).
- Same page: a fresh-context reviewer "sees only the diff and the criteria you give it, not the reasoning that produced the change"; "A fresh context improves code review since Claude won't be biased toward code it just wrote" (writer/reviewer pattern); a verification subagent has "a fresh model try to refute the result, so the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it" — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). HIGH (vendor guidance; no measured effect size given).
- Self-preference: GPT-4 and Llama 2 can distinguish their own outputs from other LLMs' and humans' above chance without fine-tuning, and fine-tuning shows a linear relation between self-recognition and strength of self-preference — Panickssery, Bowman, Feng, [arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076). HIGH (abstract level).
- Wataoka et al. (NeurIPS 2024 Safe GenAI workshop): GPT-4 shows significant self-preference bias; evaluators score lower-perplexity (more "familiar") text higher than humans do, whether or not self-generated — [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819). HIGH (abstract level; workshop paper).
- Xu et al., "Pride and Prejudice": self-bias occurs in all six examined LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Gemini, LLaMA2, Mixtral, DeepSeek) across translation, constrained generation and math; self-refine improves fluency and understandability but "further amplifies self-bias"; larger models and external feedback with accurate assessment reduce it and give real gains — [arXiv 2402.11436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436). HIGH (abstract level; preprint).
- Pan et al., "Spontaneous Reward Hacking in Iterative Self-Refinement": in essay editing, evaluator scores diverge from human judgment as iterations proceed; severity depends on model size and on whether generator and evaluator share context — [arXiv 2407.04549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549). HIGH (abstract level; no effect sizes read).
- Sycophancy (Sharma et al., Anthropic): five assistants show sycophancy across four free-form tasks; they give more positive feedback on text a user says they wrote or like; human and preference-model judgments sometimes favor convincingly written sycophantic responses over correct ones — [arXiv 2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548). HIGH.
- Answer flipping when challenged ("Are you sure?"): models admit mistakes at rates from 42% (GPT-4) to 98% (Claude 1.3) and change the initial answer at rates from 32% (GPT-4) to 86% (Claude 1.3); swaying is "not limited to answers the LM has low confidence in" — [Sharma et al. full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548). HIGH (tool-extracted) but the models are 2023 vintage (claude-1.3, claude-2.0, gpt-3.5-turbo, gpt-4, llama-2-70b-chat); no current-model numbers were found.
- Huang et al. flip analysis: GPT-3.5 on GSM8K kept the initial answer 74.7% of the time and among changed answers correct->incorrect flips were more common than incorrect->correct; Llama-2 frequently turned correct answers wrong; GPT-4 and GPT-4-Turbo kept initial answers more often. On CommonSenseQA GPT-3.5 flipped often because distractor options "often appear somewhat relevant" — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Autoreason (NousResearch; secondary summary page, authors/date not shown): names three degradation mechanisms of iterative self-refinement: prompt bias (adversarial critique prompts invent problems), scope creep across revision passes, and models rarely choosing to leave good output alone; proposes fresh Critic/Author/Synthesizer agents with no shared history and blind judging where the unchanged incumbent is a candidate and ties go to it — [HyperAI summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/autoreason). LOW (secondary listing, not independently verified or peer-reviewed).
- CodeRabbit field study: of 31,073 review/feedback pairs on 10,191 PRs in 239 repos, 36.4% of AI review comments were accepted, 7.3% triggered discussion, 56.3% were rejected; rejections were mainly "invalid suggestions" (false positives, redundancy, out-of-scope) — [arXiv 2607.03316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03316). HIGH (abstract level; preprint dated July 2026).

### Inferences
- "Provenance framing" is a lever. Self-Correction Bench shows the same error is fixed more often when it appears as the user's content than as the model's own output; Sharma et al. show positive-feedback bias when the author is claimed to be the user. The two point in opposite directions, so the safe framing is neutral third-party ("a change submitted for review"), not "your work" and not "the user's work". MEDIUM (inference from two HIGH sources).
- A critic prompt should be shaped so that "no issues found" is a legal, low-cost output. Evidence is indirect (Claude Code docs, Autoreason summary, CodeRabbit 56% rejection, CriticGPT hallucinated bugs). MEDIUM.
- Self-preference is driven by self-recognition and familiarity, so a fresh context of the same model reduces shared-context contamination (Pan) but does not remove model-level self-preference (Panickssery, Wataoka). A different or stronger reviewer is better when available (Olausson, Xu). MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found comparing "find N problems" prompts with "is there a problem?" / verdict prompts; the false-positive pressure of the former is supported only by vendor guidance, a secondary summary, and field-acceptance rates.
- No study found that directly measures fresh-subagent-same-model vs. same-context review for Claude-class agents; the Claude Code docs state the benefit without numbers.
- Sycophancy and flipping numbers come from 2023 models.
- Other claimed numbers (12 of 13 code-review agents under 60% actionable comments, arXiv 2604.03196; developers adopt AI suggestions 16.6% vs 56.5% for human suggestions, arXiv 2603.15911) appeared only in a secondary search summary and were not opened, so they are excluded.

## 3. How many iterations help; evidence of over-editing, quality drift, "making it worse" in code and prose

### Takeaway
Gains arrive in the first one to three rounds and shrink fast; with weak or no external feedback, further rounds are neutral to harmful, and errors in the context can propagate into later attempts. Multi-turn drift, reward hacking against a same-model judge, and code growth with little semantic change are documented failure modes.

### Cited Findings
- Self-Refine iteration curve (max 4 iterations, averaged over models, tool-extracted): Code Optimization 22.0 -> 27.0 -> 27.9 -> 28.8; Sentiment Reversal 33.9 -> 34.9 -> 36.1 -> 36.8; Constrained Generation 29.0 -> 40.3 -> 46.7 -> 49.7. Gains shrink each iteration; for Acronym Generation one criterion can drop while another rises, so the method keeps the best-scoring output across iterations rather than the last — [Self-Refine full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- CRITIC: improvement is continuous with diminishing returns and "2-3 rounds of corrections yield most of the benefits"; without reliable feedback, iterating on the model's own critique gives "inferior and relatively inefficient returns" — [CRITIC full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.11738). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Huang et al.: round 2 was worse than round 1 for GPT-4 on GSM8K (91.5 -> 89.0) and HotpotQA (49.0 -> 43.0) and for Llama-2 on GSM8K (43.5 -> 36.5) — [Huang et al. full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Olausson et al.: once the cost of the repair step is counted, self-repair gains in code are modest and sometimes disappear — [arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896). HIGH.
- Contextual Drag (Cheng, Zhu, Zhao, Arora; arXiv Feb 2026, v2 Mar 2026): across 11 models and 8 reasoning tasks, failed attempts left in context cause 10-20% performance drops by steering later outputs toward structurally similar mistakes; models with severe drag show "self-deterioration" over iterative self-refinement rounds; external feedback and successful self-verification did not eliminate the effect; fine-tuning and context denoising only partly helped — [arXiv 2602.04288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04288). HIGH (abstract level; preprint).
- Laban et al.: 200,000+ simulated conversations; all tested LLMs drop on average 39% in multi-turn vs. single-turn; the drop is mostly increased unreliability; "when LLMs take a wrong turn in a conversation, they get lost and do not recover" — [arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120). HIGH (abstract level; preprint).
- Javaji et al., "Another Turn, Better Output?" (12-turn iterative prompting on ideation, code, math): gains arrive early for ideas and code; after the first few turns vague feedback "often plateaus or reduces correctness"; targeted prompts shift the intended quality axis; code grows in size with little semantic change — [arXiv 2509.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06770). HIGH (abstract level; no effect sizes read).
- Pan et al. (essay editing): same-model evaluator ratings rise while human-judged quality stalls or falls (reward hacking arises spontaneously in context), worse when generator and evaluator share context — [arXiv 2407.04549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549). HIGH (abstract level).
- Xu et al.: self-refine raises fluency/understandability while amplifying self-bias, so a prose-quality gain measured by the same model can be illusory — [arXiv 2402.11436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436). HIGH (abstract level).
- Claude Code docs: "If you've corrected Claude more than twice on the same issue in one session, the context is cluttered with failed approaches. Run /clear and start fresh"; "A clean session with a better prompt almost always outperforms a long session with accumulated corrections"; chasing every reviewer finding leads to over-engineering — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). HIGH (vendor guidance).
- Autoreason (secondary summary): standard refinement baselines pushed Haiku 3.5 outputs below its single-pass quality; benefit peaked at mid-tier models; on Haiku 4.5 code, a 4-point gain on visible tests but no gain on held-out private tests — [HyperAI summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/autoreason). LOW (secondary; not independently verified).

### Inferences
- A defensible default is one review round, a second only if the first produced confirmed, fixed findings and checks are still failing, and a hard stop at three rounds or two consecutive no-new-confirmed-findings. This follows the Self-Refine/CRITIC curves and Huang round-2 declines. MEDIUM (the exact numbers are a design choice, not measured for Claude Code).
- Over-editing risk in code is supported by the code-growth finding (Javaji), reviewer-driven over-engineering (Claude Code docs), and out-of-scope review comments being a rejection driver (CodeRabbit). No paper in this search measured "regression introduced by a review pass" directly for agent-written diffs. MEDIUM.
- Because failed attempts in context bias later attempts (Contextual Drag), a revision should be built from the original task plus the verified findings, not from a transcript of the previous rounds. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study on prose over-editing by an LLM editor beyond Pan et al.'s essay setting (abstract only) and Xu et al.
- Javaji et al. and Pan et al. effect sizes were not read.
- Autoreason claims remain unverified; authors and date were not recoverable from the listing.

## 4. Coding agents with test feedback, review-agent studies, and test-time-compute / verifier results (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Execution feedback is the reliable lever; self-written tests and model-written verdicts add value only when they are checked or combined with other signals. Verifiers help best as selectors over multiple candidates and when execution-based and execution-free signals are combined; the generation-verification gap remains large, and AI review comments are mostly rejected in the field.

### Cited Findings
- Reflexion on code uses self-generated unit tests (up to 6 per problem, AST-checked): HumanEval Python 91.0 vs 80.1 (GPT-4), Rust (50 hardest) 68.0 vs 60.0, but MBPP Python 77.1 vs 80.1 (GPT-4, i.e. worse). The authors attribute the MBPP shortfall to test false positives: 16.3% on MBPP Python vs 1.4% on HumanEval Python — [Reflexion full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Kamoi et al. treat code-interpreter feedback (Self-Debug, CRITIC) as a fair, reliable setting and report negative results for intrinsic self-correction in code (citing Gou et al., Olausson et al.) — [Kamoi et al. HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01297v3). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Olausson et al.: see section 2 (feedback is the bottleneck; stronger-model or human feedback helps most) — [arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896). HIGH.
- Anthropic: agents must "gain 'ground truth' from the environment at each step" — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents). HIGH.
- Claude Code docs: the single most useful thing is to "Give Claude a check it can run: tests, a build, a screenshot to compare"; options for gating completion include a Stop hook running a deterministic script, a `/goal` condition re-checked by a separate evaluator, and a verification subagent; "Have Claude show evidence rather than asserting success: the test output, the command it ran and what it returned"; a "trust-then-verify gap" is a listed failure pattern; the bundled `/code-review` skill reviews the diff "in a fresh subagent" — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). HIGH (vendor guidance).
- R2E-Gym (2025): execution-based (test) verifiers have low distinguishability and execution-free verifiers are biased and rely on stylistic features; each alone saturates around 42-43%, and a hybrid reached 51% on SWE-bench Verified — summary in [search result listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07164) (paper itself not opened). MEDIUM.
- SWE-RM (Dec 2025) argues test-time-scaling gains alone do not measure a verifier; accuracy and calibration both matter — [arXiv 2512.21919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21919) (not opened; claim from search summary). MEDIUM.
- SWE-ZERO/SWE-HERO reports a margin up to 15% at K=16 between verifier-selected Best@K and the Pass@K ceiling, i.e. open-source verifiers do not fully exploit sampling — [arXiv 2604.01496](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01496) (not opened; claim from search summary). MEDIUM.
- DeepSWE-Verifier (fine-tuned Qwen3-14B critic) paired with the agent raises SWE-bench Verified by about 10% — [Friendli model page](https://friendli.ai/models/agentica-org/DeepSWE-Verifier). MEDIUM (secondary).
- Weaver (Stanford, NeurIPS 2025): general LM judges and reward models trail oracle verifiers; weak-supervision ensembles of cheap verifiers lifted Llama 3.3 70B to 86.2% average accuracy on reasoning/math tasks — [Stanford Scaling Intelligence](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/weaver); see also [NeurIPS listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117007). MEDIUM (secondary summary of authors' own results; no independent replication found).
- Snell, Lee, Xu, Kumar (2024): effectiveness of test-time-compute strategies (verifier/PRM search vs. adaptive revision of the response distribution) depends on prompt difficulty; a compute-optimal allocation improves efficiency over best-of-N by more than 4x, and a smaller model with test-time compute can beat a 14x larger model on problems where the small model has non-trivial success — [arXiv 2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314). HIGH (abstract level; revision-vs-parallel numbers not read).
- Self-Correction Bench: reasoning (RL-trained) models show little or no blind spot; "Wait" lifted DeepSeek-V3 macro-average accuracy from 0.578 to 0.918, near DeepSeek-R1-0528's 0.908 — [arXiv 2507.02778 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02778v2). HIGH (tool-extracted; injected-error setup).
- CodeRabbit field study: 36.4% accepted, 56.3% rejected — [arXiv 2607.03316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03316). HIGH (abstract level).
- Contextual Drag: external feedback did not eliminate self-deterioration in models with severe drag — [arXiv 2602.04288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04288). HIGH (abstract level).

### Inferences
- For a post-task skill in a coding session the ranking of signals is: executed ground truth (tests, build, type check, lint, running the app) > independent-context reviewer with an evidence requirement > same-context self-review. The first two are directly supported by Reflexion/CRITIC/Kamoi and by Claude Code guidance; the ordering of the second over the third is MEDIUM.
- Agent-written tests are a signal with a measurable false-positive rate (16.3% on MBPP); a skill that lets the agent write its own check should treat a pass as weak evidence and prefer pre-existing tests or checks derived from the user's stated acceptance criteria. MEDIUM.
- Hybrid signals beat single signals (R2E-Gym, Weaver): run several cheap independent checks (tests + type check + lint + reviewer verdict) rather than one. MEDIUM (secondary sources).
- 2025-2026 test-time-compute work is mostly about selecting among sampled candidates; it supports using a verifier to pick or reject, not asking the generator to rewrite its own answer. A skill that does one generation plus one review is a small instance of the same idea. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of Claude Code/SWE-bench-style agents running an end-of-task self-review pass and its net effect on resolved rate was found in this search. Evidence is analogical (Reflexion, Self-Debug/CRITIC, verifiers).
- R2E-Gym, SWE-RM, SWE-ZERO/HERO, DeepSWE-Verifier and Weaver figures come from search summaries, not from the papers; they should be re-checked before being quoted as hard numbers.
- OpenAI/DeepMind engineering posts on reviewer/verifier agents were not located in this search; only the OpenAI CriticGPT paper was read.
- Snell et al.'s revisions-vs-parallel-sampling comparison numbers were not read (the abstract page reports none).

## 5. Net design implications: 12 falsifiable design rules for a post-task refine/verify skill

### Takeaway
Build the skill as an evidence-gated verification pass, not a "think again and improve" pass: run external checks first, review in a fresh context against a closed rubric derived from the original request, accept only confirmed and in-scope findings, keep a regression gate, and stop after at most two or three rounds. "No change needed" must be a first-class outcome.

### Cited Findings
- Rules below are inferences; their supporting sources are cited inline and in sections 1-4. See Inferences for the list.

### Inferences
Each rule lists supporting sources, a confidence label, and the observation that would falsify it.

1. **Run external checks first and gate every revision on their output.** Execute whatever ground truth exists (tests, build, type check, linter, running the artifact, fetching cited URLs, screenshot diff) before any model critique; a revision is justified by a failing check or a confirmed rubric violation, not by the model's impression. Prefer checks the generator did not write or has not seen. Support: [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798), [Kamoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297), [CRITIC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738), [Stechly et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08115), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents), [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366) (self-written tests had 16.3% false positives on MBPP). Confidence HIGH. Falsified if, on a benchmark with tests, a no-tools critique-and-revise pass matches the tool-grounded pass in resolved rate at equal cost.

2. **Make the first pass strong and measure the review pass against an equal-compute baseline.** Requirements, constraints and acceptance criteria belong in the initial prompt/plan; the review pass must not be where they are first applied, and its benefit must be judged against "just do the first pass well" and, for reasoning answers, against majority vote at equal calls. Support: [Huang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798) (81.8 baseline with explicit instruction vs 75.1 after self-correction; self-consistency 88.2 vs debate 83.0), [Kamoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01297v3). Confidence HIGH. Falsified if the review pass raises quality over a strong first-pass prompt with the same number of model calls on held-out tasks.

3. **Review in a fresh context, with only the artifact, the original request and the rubric, and frame it as third-party work.** Do not pass the generator's reasoning or earlier failed attempts; do not say "you wrote this" or "the user wrote this". Support: [Self-Correction Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02778v2), [Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549), [Contextual Drag](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04288), [Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). Confidence MEDIUM (direct fresh-subagent-vs-same-context measurement for Claude-class agents not found). Falsified if seeded-defect detection and false-positive rates are equal for same-context and fresh-subagent review.

4. **Prefer a different or stronger reviewer when one is available; treat same-model fresh context as the floor.** Same-model critics recognize and favor their own text. Support: [Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076), [Wataoka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819), [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436), [Olausson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896), [Weaver](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/weaver). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if a same-model fresh-context reviewer detects seeded defects in the model's own output as often as a stronger/different-family reviewer.

5. **Give the reviewer a closed rubric derived from the user's request, ask for a per-item verdict, and make "no issues" a legal answer; never use quotas such as "find at least N problems".** Restrict findings to correctness and stated requirements. Support: [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) (reviewer finds gaps even when work is sound), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) (clear evaluation criteria), [Javaji et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06770) (vague feedback plateaus or reduces correctness), [CodeRabbit study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03316) (56.3% rejected), [Autoreason summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/autoreason) (LOW). Confidence MEDIUM (no controlled test of quota vs. verdict prompts found). Falsified if, on already-correct artifacts, a verdict prompt yields as many accepted edits and the same quality as a "find problems" prompt.

6. **Require every finding to carry evidence: location, a concrete failing scenario or command, expected vs. actual, and severity; drop or tag "unverified" any finding without it.** Specific, localized feedback is what makes correction work; unevidenced critiques hallucinate. Support: [Self-Refine ablation](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651), [Tyen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516), [Olausson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896), [CriticGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215) (hallucinated bugs). Confidence MEDIUM-HIGH. Falsified if evidence-free findings are accepted/confirmed at the same rate as evidence-bearing ones.

7. **Verify before fixing, then regression-gate the fix.** Reproduce each finding (failing check before, passing after); keep the revision only if everything that passed before still passes plus the targeted finding now passes; otherwise revert that change. Support: [Tyen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516) (finding is the bottleneck), [Reflexion](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366) (false positives cause bad submissions), [Huang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798) (correct->incorrect flips), [Self-Refine](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651) (keeps best-scoring output rather than last). Confidence HIGH for the gate, MEDIUM for the exact mechanics. Falsified if ungated revisions show no more regressions than gated ones.

8. **Cap rounds: one review round by default, a second only if round one produced confirmed fixes and checks still fail; hard stop at three; stop early on two consecutive rounds with no confirmed finding; after two failed corrections restart from the original task plus verified findings in clean context rather than appending to the transcript.** Support: [Self-Refine curves](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651), [CRITIC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.11738) (2-3 rounds), [Huang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798) (round 2 worse), [Contextual Drag](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04288), [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). Confidence HIGH for "few rounds", MEDIUM for the exact cap. Falsified if quality keeps rising monotonically through round 4+ with tool-grounded feedback in the target task set.

9. **Lock the scope: the review pass may change only what the original request and confirmed findings cover; out-of-scope ideas are listed, not applied.** Support: [Javaji et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06770) (code grows with little semantic change), [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) (over-engineering), [CodeRabbit study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03316) (scope problems among rejections), [Autoreason summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/autoreason) (LOW). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if diff size and unrequested edits do not differ between scope-locked and unlocked passes, or unrequested edits correlate with higher acceptance.

10. **Never use the model's own numeric self-score or "looks good" as the stop criterion.** Stop on external checks passing plus every rubric item satisfied by cited evidence. Support: [Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549), [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436), [Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076), [Self-Refine math null](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651) (94% "everything looks good"). Confidence HIGH. Falsified if same-model scores track human or ground-truth quality across iterations on the target tasks.

11. **Route by task type.** Where checking is cheaper than generating (executable behavior, format/constraints, requirement coverage, citations, decomposable claims) run the verification pass. Where correctness has no external check (pure reasoning, open-ended analysis), do independent re-derivation and compare answers (self-consistency style) or escalate to the user instead of asking the same model to critique itself. Support: [Kamoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01297v3), [Huang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.01798), [Snell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314) (difficulty-dependent). Confidence MEDIUM-HIGH. Falsified if no-check reasoning tasks gain from critique-and-revise at equal cost against majority vote.

12. **Scale review effort to risk and difficulty, and report evidence in the output (checks run, results, findings confirmed/rejected, what changed, or "no change needed").** Skip or shrink the pass for trivial, low-risk tasks; after cost is counted, repair gains are often modest. Support: [Olausson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896), [Snell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314), [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) ("show evidence rather than asserting success"). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if an always-on full review pass shows better cost-adjusted outcomes than a risk-gated pass on a mixed task set.

### Gaps
- None of the 12 rules was tested end-to-end in a Claude Code skill; they are derived from model-level and agent-level studies, mostly on 2023-2025 models. A small A/B (with and without the skill, on seeded-defect tasks and clean tasks) is the direct way to falsify or confirm rules 3, 5 and 8.
- Rule 4 (different/stronger reviewer) depends on what models the skill's users can run; the marketplace skill may only have same-model subagents available.
- Rule thresholds (one round default, cap three) are design choices anchored to the Self-Refine and CRITIC curves, not measured values for Claude Code.
