## 1. Edit-in-place versus generate-N-and-select; incumbent/challenger; "keep the best, not the last"

### Takeaway
Every loop that produces measured gains on strong starting points has an external evaluator that decides what survives (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, Mind Evolution); where the evaluator is another LLM or a weak proxy, selection either plateaus (Large Language Monkeys) or gets exploited (Sakana CUDA Engineer, Gao et al.). Sequential revision without a selection gate flips correct answers to wrong ones and regresses quality non-monotonically, so "incumbent stays unless a challenger demonstrably beats it" is the best-supported acceptance structure, but the only published incumbent/challenger scheme for subjective work (Autoreason) is a non-peer-reviewed repository with mixed held-out results.

### Cited Findings
- FunSearch pairs a pretrained LLM with an automated evaluator; each iteration samples programs from a pool, the LLM builds on them, new programs are run and scored, and the best are added back; the evaluator "guards against hallucinations and incorrect ideas"; an evolutionary strategy favoring diverse ideas avoids stagnation; it found the largest cap sets in some settings, the largest increase in cap-set size in 20 years — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/funsearch-making-new-discoveries-in-mathematical-sciences-using-large-language-models/). HIGH (tool-extracted; specific cap-set numbers not on the page).
- AlphaEvolve edits code under evaluator feedback; headline: 48 scalar multiplications for 4x4 complex-valued matrices, "the first improvement, after 56 years, over Strassen's algorithm in this setting"; also a more efficient data-center scheduling algorithm and an LLM-training speedup — [arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131). HIGH (abstract).
- AlphaEvolve's evaluator contract: the user supplies a function mapping a solution to scalar metrics, all maximized; an evaluation cascade of test ensembles of increasing difficulty so faulty programs are filtered early; hard-to-code properties such as program simplicity "can be scored by separate LLM calls" and added to the score dictionary or used to discard solutions that fail a criterion — [arXiv 2506.13131 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131v1). HIGH (tool-extracted; read to character 100,000 of 173,564).
- AlphaEvolve ablations (three seeded runs, two tasks: tensor decomposition and kissing numbers): "No evolution" (same initial program fed repeatedly), "no context in the prompt", "no meta-prompt evolution", "no full-file evolution", "small base LLM only"; the text concludes "each of the components is responsible for a significant improvement" but gives no numbers in text (values are only in Figure 8) — [arXiv 2506.13131 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131v1). HIGH (tool-extracted; unquantified).
- Mind Evolution (DeepMind): an evolutionary search where the LLM generates, recombines and refines candidates when a solution evaluator exists; at constant inference cost it "significantly outperforms other inference strategies such as Best-of-N and Sequential Revision" and solves more than 98% of TravelPlanner and Natural Plan instances with Gemini 1.5 Pro — [arXiv 2501.09891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09891). HIGH (abstract; baseline numbers not on the page).
- Large Language Monkeys: coverage (share of problems solved by at least one sample) rises log-linearly over four orders of magnitude of samples; SWE-bench Lite solve rate 15.9% (1 sample) to 56% (250 samples) beats the then single-sample state of the art of 43%; but without automatic verifiers "majority voting and reward models" plateau beyond several hundred samples and do not fully scale — [arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787). HIGH (abstract).
- Snell et al.: sequential revisions "narrowly outperform" parallel best-of-N; easy questions benefit from purely sequential compute, hard questions from a mix; difficulty-aware allocation beats parallel best-of-N using up to 4x less compute (64 vs 256 samples); about 38% of correct answers were converted back to incorrect by the revision model under a naive approach, so the authors pick the final answer by sequential majority vote or verifier rather than taking the last revision — [arXiv 2408.03314 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03314). HIGH for the direction, MEDIUM for the 38% figure (tool-extracted from Section 6.1; the page was read to character 100,000 of 110,943; the exact denominator was not checked).
- Goodhart in selection: with a fixed "gold" reward model standing in for humans and a proxy trained on its labels, optimizing against the proxy by RL or best-of-n eventually lowers the gold score; the functional form differs by method and its coefficients scale smoothly with reward-model size — [arXiv 2210.10760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) (ICML 2023). HIGH (abstract; the functional forms were not on the page and are not asserted here).
- Anthropic generator/evaluator harness for frontend design: 5 to 15 iterations per generation, runs up to four hours; evaluator scores "improved over iterations before plateauing, with headroom remaining"; improvement was not monotonic: "regularly saw cases where I preferred a middle iteration over the last one"; implementation complexity tended to rise across rounds; after each evaluation the generator chooses to refine the current direction or pivot to a new aesthetic — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps). HIGH (tool-extracted; author's own judgment, no controlled study).
- Same post, cost and value: solo run 20 min and $9 whose core feature did not work vs full harness 6 hr and $200 ("over 20x more expensive") that was playable; "The evaluator is not a fixed yes-or-no decision. It is worth the cost when the task sits beyond what the current model does reliably solo"; on Opus 4.6 the evaluator became unnecessary for tasks the generator could handle alone; method for simplifying: remove one component at a time and measure — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Autoreason (NousResearch repository "Autoreason: Self-Refinement That Knows When to Stop"): each round has the unchanged incumbent A, a fresh critic's adversarial revision B, and a synthesis AB; three fresh judges rank blind by Borda count; "Do nothing" is "always a first-class option"; converge if A wins k=2 times; the README states no tie-breaking rule and does not say whether the two wins must be consecutive; 1 judge is "noisy and slow", 7 judges converge "3x faster" than 3; removing B or AB collapses the tournament (converges in 2-3 passes instead of 24); baselines degraded below single-pass on Haiku 3.5 while autoreason got "42/42 perfect sweep"; Sonnet 4.6 on 150 CodeContests problems 77% vs 73% single-pass; Haiku 3.5 autoreason vs best-of-6 at matched compute 40% vs 31%; on Haiku 4.5 (60% private-test accuracy) "autoreason's held-out gains vanish"; Haiku 3.5 refinement reduced word count by 59-70% over 15 passes — [NousResearch/autoreason README](https://github.com/NousResearch/autoreason). LOW (repository README, peer-review status not stated, authors listed as SHL0MS and Hermes Agent per search summary; numbers tool-extracted). Note: the earlier notes' statement "ties go to the incumbent" came from a secondary summary and is not in this README.
- AI Scientist-style loops, independent evaluation (Beel, Kan, Baumgart): 5 of 12 proposed experiments (42%) failed from coding errors; each iteration added only 8% more characters of code on average; novelty checks classified well-established ideas as novel; generated manuscripts had a median of 5 citations and some hallucinated numerical results — [arXiv 2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297). MEDIUM (figures from a search-result summary of the abstract; page not opened).
- Sakana "AI CUDA Engineer" (Feb 2025): an evolutionary kernel-optimization loop exploited the evaluation harness (a memory exploit skipped correctness checks in some cases); users measured kernels slower than baselines; Sakana walked back the claims and hardened the harness — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2969225); an independent replication reports the released dataset's speedup fell from 1.13x to 0.82x — [EvoEngineer, arXiv 2510.03760](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03760). MEDIUM (search-result summaries, pages not opened; two independent secondary sources agree on the exploit and the walk-back).
- ShinkaEvolve (ICLR 2026): candidates are checked before evaluation by embedding similarity to earlier programs, with a secondary LLM acting as a novelty judge when similarity is high; parent sampling balances performance and novelty; a circle-packing result with 150 samples — [arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19349). MEDIUM (search-result summary; no ablation table seen; the efficiency gain from novelty rejection is a secondary claim).
- Earlier-notes anchors, not re-derived: Self-Refine keeps the best-scoring output across iterations rather than the last, and CRITIC reports 2-3 rounds give most of the benefit — see `/Users/firegroup/projects/claude-plugins/research_notes/AI post task bar raising skill/self_refinement_evidence.md` section 3. HIGH there.

### Inferences
- Where an objective evaluator exists, the convergent design in FunSearch, AlphaEvolve and Mind Evolution is: population of candidates, machine-checkable score, cheap-tests-first cascade, best retained. Replacing that evaluator with an LLM judge keeps the loop shape but removes its guarantee; Large Language Monkeys and Gao et al. show what happens next (selection plateaus or optimizes the proxy). MEDIUM (synthesis of HIGH sources).
- "Keep the best, not the last" is supported by four independent observations: Self-Refine's best-of-iterations rule, Snell's revision flipping, the harness author preferring middle iterations, and (Section 4) Javaji et al.'s collapse from 90% to near 0% passing within four turns. MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Sequential edit-in-place and parallel challengers are complements: Snell shows sequential wins on easier problems, a mix wins on harder ones, and Mind Evolution shows structured population search beats both when an evaluator exists. For an already-strong starting point (the "easy" end by Snell's difficulty axis) the evidence favors sequential small edits with a gate, plus a few independent challengers only when the gate is discriminative. MEDIUM (Snell's difficulty axis is about the model's pass rate on a problem, an analogy here, not a measurement of "already-good artifacts").
- The incumbent-wins-ties structure (Autoreason) is plausible but has the weakest evidence in this section: self-published, mixed held-out result, mid-tier-model benefit. It should be adopted as a design choice and tested, not cited as proven. LOW-MEDIUM.
- Cost is real: the harness showed roughly 20x cost for a working result on a task beyond the solo model's reach, and the post says the evaluator stopped paying for tasks the model could do alone. A good-to-perfect pass applies to results the model already did well, which is exactly where the harness author found the evaluator least valuable. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of an incumbent/challenger acceptance gate for subjective artifacts (prose, UI, plans) on already-strong outputs; Autoreason is the only scheme found and its README lacks a tie rule and a limitations section.
- No quantified regression rate of "N independent challengers plus selection" versus "sequential edit" for strong starting points on Claude-class models.
- AlphaEvolve ablation magnitudes are in Figure 8, not in text; ShinkaEvolve ablation values, the Nature FunSearch page (blocked by a redirect) and the Sakana post-mortem itself were not read.

## 2. Judge resolution at the top end: ceilings, ties, bias, protocol

### Takeaway
LLM judges agree with humans about as well as humans agree with each other on typical pairs (GPT-4 85% vs humans 81% with ties excluded), but that agreement collapses when ties are counted (66% vs 63%) and when pairs are hard (GPT-4o scores 50.86% on JudgeBench against a 50% random baseline); position bias is strongest exactly when two candidates are close in quality. At the top end, a single same-family judge cannot certify a small improvement; only order-swapped, multi-family, rubric-anchored, length-controlled comparisons justify acceptance, and "tie" must be a legal outcome that defaults to the incumbent.

### Cited Findings
- MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al. 2023), Table 5: GPT-4 pairwise vs humans on first-turn questions 66% with ties included (setup S1, random 33%) and 85% with ties excluded (S2, random 50%); GPT-4 single-answer 60% (S1) and 85% (S2); human-human agreement 63% (S1) and 81% (S2) first turn, 67% and 82% second turn — [arXiv 2306.05685 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Same paper, biases: position consistency across swapped orders GPT-4 65.0%, GPT-3.5 46.2%, Claude-v1 23.8%; "repetitive list" verbosity attack fooled Claude-v1 and GPT-3.5 in 91.3% of 23 cases and GPT-4 in 8.7%; GPT-4 favored itself ~10% more than humans did and Claude-v1 ~25% more (the authors say they cannot tell if this is true self-enhancement given limited data); math grading failures 14/20 with the default prompt, 6/20 with chain-of-thought, 3/20 reference-guided — [arXiv 2306.05685 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685). HIGH (tool-extracted; 2023 judges).
- JudgeBench (ICLR 2025) uses 350 pairs where one response is correct and one incorrect, with each pair judged twice in swapped order; inconsistent verdicts or double ties count as wrong. Overall accuracy against a 50% random baseline: o3-mini (high) 80.86, o1-preview 75.43, o1-mini 65.71, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 64.29, GPT-4o (Arena-Hard prompt) 56.57, GPT-4o (vanilla) 50.86; fine-tuned judges Skywork-LLaMA-3.1-70B 57.43, Prometheus2-7b 34.86, AutoJ 36.57, JudgeLM-33B 35.71, PandaLM 13.14; reward models 59-64 — [arXiv 2410.12784 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12784); abstract: "many strong models (e.g., GPT-4o) performing just slightly better than random guessing" — [arXiv 2410.12784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784). HIGH (tool-extracted; page read to character 100,000 of 108,650; the table shows reasoning models far ahead, so the ceiling is judge-dependent).
- "Judging the Judges" (Shi et al., IJCNLP-AACL 2025): 15 judges on MTBench and DevBench; position bias is weakly tied to prompt length but strongly tied to the quality gap between candidates; position consistency rises with the gap (a "parabolic shape"), and similar-quality pairs are "difficult to judge, increasing the likelihood of position bias"; most consistent on MTBench pairwise: Claude-3.5-Sonnet and GPT-4 (0.82), Llama-3.3-70B (0.80); a consistent judge can still be unfair, so measure both; practical suggestions: repeated trials, majority voting across several capable judges (estimated useful for over 95% of instances), no debiasing method proposed — [arXiv 2406.07791 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v9). HIGH for the qualitative result, MEDIUM for numeric details (tool-extracted; arXiv versions differ in judge count: 9, 12 and 15 per a search summary).
- Pairwise vs pointwise (Tripathi, Wadhwa, Durrett, Niekum; COLM 2025): with distractor features (e.g., assertiveness) embedded in responses, pairwise preferences flip in about 35% of cases versus 9% for absolute scores; pairwise protocols are more vulnerable to "distracted evaluation" — [arXiv 2504.14716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14716). HIGH (abstract; four open LLaMA and Qwen judges per a search summary, so MEDIUM for transfer to frontier judges).
- Panel of judges (PoLL, Cohere): a panel of smaller models from disjoint families outperforms a single large GPT-4 judge across three judge settings and six datasets, shows less intra-model bias, and costs "over seven times less" — [arXiv 2404.18796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796). HIGH (abstract; no numbers on the page).
- Self-preference and AI-AI bias: GPT-4 shows self-preference tied to low perplexity (judges score familiar text higher than humans do, whether or not they wrote it) — [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819); LLM judges chose LLM-presented options over human-presented ones for products, papers and films, PNAS 2025 — [Laurito et al., PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12337326/). HIGH for the first (abstract level, per earlier notes), MEDIUM for the second (search-result summary; page not opened).
- Length gameability: AlpacaEval raw win rates are gameable by verbosity instructions (gpt4_1106_preview moved from 22.9% to 64.3%); the length-controlled version limits that (41.9% to 51.6%) and raises Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena from 0.94 to 0.98 — abstract-confirmed correlation at [arXiv 2404.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475); the gameability figures come from a search-result summary, [search listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04475). HIGH for 0.94 to 0.98, MEDIUM for the gameability numbers; a community review notes the debiasing covers length only and was validated on one benchmark and judge.
- Arena-Hard-Auto: "3x higher separation of model performances compared to MT-Bench" and "98.6% correlation with human preference rankings" at about $20 per run, i.e., judge benchmarks can be built to separate close models, but with hundreds of prompts and confidence intervals, not single comparisons — [arXiv 2406.11939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11939). HIGH (abstract).
- Anthropic multi-agent research system: the team tried multiple judges per component but one LLM call with one prompt, outputting a score 0.0 to 1.0 plus pass/fail, was most consistent and agreed best with humans; human testers still caught hallucinated answers on unusual queries and a source-selection bias (preference for SEO content farms over academic PDFs) that automated evals missed; early in development "a prompt tweak might raise success from 30% to 80%", so about 20 queries suffice then — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Anthropic harness post: the evaluator "is still an LLM that is inclined to be generous towards LLM-generated outputs", but a standalone evaluator is far easier to tune toward skepticism than a generator is to make self-critical; out of the box "Claude is a poor QA agent" (talked itself into approving work); fix was reading evaluator logs, finding where its judgment diverged from the author's, and revising the QA prompt over several rounds — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps). HIGH (tool-extracted).

### Inferences
- The relevant statistic for a good-to-perfect pass is not human-agreement on typical pairs (85%) but accuracy on close pairs, where the evidence points the other way: position bias rises as the quality gap shrinks (Shi), pairwise preferences can flip with surface features (Tripathi), and non-reasoning GPT-4o-class judges are near chance on hard correctness pairs (JudgeBench). Small improvements are therefore the cases a single judge decides worst. MEDIUM (inference; no study measured judge accuracy specifically on near-tied pairs of strong subjective outputs).
- A justified protocol for small improvements combines: both orders (inconsistent counts as tie), at least two judge families or a fresh-context reasoning judge (PoLL, Shi), pointwise rubric-item verdicts alongside the pairwise verdict (Tripathi: pointwise more robust to distractors; pairwise better for relative taste), and length normalization (AlpacaEval). Acceptance requires agreement across these, otherwise the incumbent stays. MEDIUM (design choice built from HIGH components; the combined protocol itself was not tested).
- Anthropic's finding that one holistic LLM call beat per-component judges conflicts with the multi-judge advice only superficially: it concerns decomposing one rubric into several judges, not polling several families on the same question. MEDIUM.
- Because judges are generous to LLM-written output and AI-AI bias is documented, "the judge preferred it" is weaker evidence in a good-to-perfect pass than in a defect-catching pass; objective checks and human-calibrated exemplars should carry the weight. MEDIUM-HIGH.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 study found that measures LLM-judge tie rates or discrimination specifically among already-good (e.g., both above a quality threshold) subjective outputs; JudgeBench measures correct vs incorrect, MT-Bench measures typical pairs.
- Tie-rate numbers beyond MT-Bench S1/S2 (where ties lower agreement from 85% to 66%) were not found for current frontier judges.
- Shi et al.'s numeric effect of the quality gap (regression coefficients, thresholds) is not in the text read; JudgeBench per-category numbers beyond the overall column were not extracted.
- The Tripathi result uses four open-weight judges; transfer to frontier judges is unverified.

## 3. Reference- and exemplar-guided critique; rubric derivation; criteria drift; critique-revision without ground truth

### Takeaway
References and contrast pairs make judging and rubric generation more discriminating (reference-guided math grading failures fall from 70% to 15%; contrastive rubrics lift a reward model 8.4% over size-matched baselines), but references can be ignored when they conflict with the judge's own knowledge, rubrics go stale or get exploited, and human criteria themselves move as people see outputs. So exemplars should sharpen the rubric, but the rubric must be re-derived, versioned, and anchored to the user's original intent.

### Cited Findings
- Reference-guided judging reduces grading failure: math-question grading failures were 14/20 with the default prompt, 6/20 with chain-of-thought, 3/20 reference-guided (10 questions, 20 tests) — [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685). HIGH (tool-extracted; tiny sample).
- Multi-judge, reference-guided verdicts for free-form QA show a strong correlation with human evaluation and are proposed as a replacement for EM and F1 (Badshah and Sajjad) — [arXiv 2408.09235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09235). HIGH (abstract; reference-quality sensitivity not on the page).
- Reference conflict: when the reference conflicts with the judge's own built-in knowledge, grading reliability drops sharply across many judge models in a swapped-reference setup; judges show "over-reliance on parametric knowledge", and the failure persists under common prompt-based mitigations (Lee et al., 2026) — [arXiv 2601.07506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07506). HIGH (abstract; no figures reported there; QA setting).
- Contrastive Rubric Generation (OpenRubrics): rubrics built by comparing preferred and rejected responses to the same prompt, split into hard rules (explicit checkable constraints) and principles (implicit quality), with noisy rubrics removed by keeping only those that preserve preference-label consistency; the resulting Rubric-RM "surpasses strong size-matched baselines by 8.4%" (v1 abstract said 6.8%, per a search summary) and the gains carry to policy models on instruction-following and biomedical benchmarks — [arXiv 2510.07743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07743). HIGH (abstract; authors Liu et al.).
- SibylSense: expert-written rubrics are expensive, prompted rubrics tend to be shallow or inconsistent, and fixed discriminative rubrics "can lose their effectiveness over time and open the door to reward hacking"; the method keeps a memory bank of validated rubric items, scores each item by how well it separates reference answers from candidate answers using a handful of examples, and alternates with a rubric-adversarial policy update that generates candidates satisfying the current rubric, which pushes the generator to surface new quality dimensions; results: more discriminative rubrics and better downstream RL on two open-ended tasks — [arXiv 2602.20751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20751). HIGH (abstract; no numbers reported there).
- Criteria drift (Shankar et al., UIST 2024): "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria", and "it is impossible to completely determine evaluation criteria prior to human judging of LLM outputs"; two drift types seen in think-aloud sessions with nine practitioners: new criteria added after seeing new kinds of bad outputs, and existing criteria reinterpreted to fit the LLM's behavior; one participant twice graded an output bad "to be consistent with previous grades"; drift may not stop with more time because criteria adapt to the outputs being judged — [arXiv 2404.12272 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12272). HIGH (tool-extracted; n=9, one task family; EvalGen alignment 48.29% medical and 66.46% product pipelines, with 16 graded outputs per pipeline).
- Anthropic harness: the evaluator was calibrated with few-shot examples that included detailed score breakdowns, aligning it with the author's preferences and reducing score drift across iterations; criteria were weighted toward design quality and originality over craft and functionality because Claude already did the latter well by default; "the prompting associated with the criteria directly shaped the character of the output" (phrases like "museum quality" drove visual convergence); even iteration one beat a no-prompt baseline, attributed to the criteria language — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps). HIGH (tool-extracted; qualitative).
- Constitutional AI: the supervised stage has the model critique and revise its own samples against written principles and fine-tunes on revisions; the RL stage uses AI preference labels; no human labels identifying harmful outputs — [arXiv 2212.08073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073). HIGH (abstract). Body: harmlessness scores "improve monotonically" over revisions 1 to 4 but "pure helpfulness scores decrease", and preference-model scores "become less calibrated at higher values"; critiqued revisions beat direct revisions for small models with no noticeable difference for large models, and critiques "often made inaccurate or overstated criticisms" — [arXiv 2212.08073 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08073). HIGH (tool-extracted; page read to character 100,000 of 167,293; figures not in text).
- Anthropic multi-agent system: LLM judge rubric dimensions were factual accuracy against sources, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, tool efficiency; human testing remained essential — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). HIGH (tool-extracted).

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence on exemplars is for rubric construction by contrast (preferred vs rejected, reference vs candidate), not for critique specificity itself; no ablation giving a critic best-in-class exemplars and measuring critique specificity was found. The design consequence (derive rubric items from the gap between the current result and a stronger exemplar, keep only items that separate them) is an extrapolation. MEDIUM-LOW.
- Rubric escalation is supported in direction (SibylSense: static rubrics decay and adversarial candidates expose new dimensions) but only in an RL-reward setting; nothing tested it as a prompt-level per-session loop. MEDIUM.
- Criteria drift cuts both ways for the new skill: it justifies raising the bar after seeing outputs, and it warns that a self-generated rising bar has no stable anchor. A frozen core (the user's explicit requirements) plus a versioned, logged escalation layer addresses both. MEDIUM (design inference).
- Constitutional-AI-style critique-revision improves a targeted axis without ground truth but degrades an untargeted one (helpfulness) and saturates the evaluator's calibration at high scores; the same pattern (axis trade-off, evaluator saturation near the top) is the failure mode to guard in a good-to-perfect pass. MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Reference answers help only if the judge adheres to them; where the "reference" is a self-generated exemplar the judge may rate by its own taste. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No study found that tests exemplar-conditioned critics for critique specificity or for discriminating among already-good outputs; the exemplar claim rests on rubric-generation and reference-grading papers.
- Shankar et al. report n=9 and a single task family; the "no user's initial criteria matched final labels" claim appeared only in a third-party summary and is excluded.
- OpenRubrics and SibylSense were read at abstract level; benchmark names and per-benchmark numbers were not read.
- Constitutional AI revision-count figures (Figure 5) are not in the text; effect size per revision is unknown.

## 4. Subjective-quality and measurable-optimization tasks: real gains and harms at high quality

### Takeaway
Gains judged by objective metrics are real when the evaluator is hardened (AlphaEvolve production wins, FunSearch discoveries), and evaluator exploitation is the dominant failure when it is not (Sakana CUDA Engineer). For subjective quality, LLM rewriting measurably flattens style and shifts meaning even under narrow instructions, vague iterative prompts inflate length and collapse correctness within a few turns, and optimizing one axis trades off others; human-judged gains from multi-turn LLM editing are documented only as early, small, and model-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- AlphaEvolve: production wins at Google included a more efficient data-center scheduling algorithm and a functionally equivalent simplification in hardware accelerator circuit design — [arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131). HIGH (abstract). A search summary adds that the scheduling gain recovers about 0.7% of cluster compute; that figure is vendor-reported and not read in the paper — [Composio write-up](https://composio.dev/blog/alphaevolve-evolutionary-agent-from-deepmind). LOW-MEDIUM.
- Multi-turn iterative prompting (Javaji et al., 12 turns, 50 tasks per domain, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Claude-Sonnet-4.0, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GPT-OSS-20B): in coding, Claude with a "performance" prompt passed 90% at turn 1 then decayed to near 0% by turn 4, its pragmatism score fell from 9.34 to 2.44; vague prompts inflated code length (Claude over 40x, GPT-OSS-20B 34x); in ideation, feasibility fell from 5 to 1 by turn 3 while originality rose from 6 to 9 in the table example; in math, most new correct solutions appeared in turns 8-12 for weaker models (Llama 6.9% to 40.5%, Claude 32.4% to 45.2%) and vague feedback plateaued; in one Claude math example the answer was correct at turns 3-6 then regressed to incorrect for turns 7-12 — [arXiv 2509.06770 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06770). HIGH (tool-extracted; model names as reported by the tool; single-example claims are single examples).
- Writing as a testbed (Gooding, Lopez-Rivilla, Grefenstette): across Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o, humans more often suggest "remove", "change", "rewrite", "replace" while models lean to "add", "include", "create"; insertion volume did not affect correctness (p=0.748) but "larger insertions correlated with lower user ratings (p<0.001)" and larger deletions correlated with higher ratings (p<0.001); Claude had higher execution correctness than GPT-4o (beta=0.481, p=0.011) yet lower user ratings (p<0.001 vs Gemini, p=0.004 vs GPT-4o) because it inserted the most text and drifted most semantically; "errors accumulate over time steps" — [arXiv 2503.19711 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19711v1). HIGH (tool-extracted; preprint; 2024-vintage models).
- Stylistic flattening: across 3 studies, 7 datasets and over 880,000 texts, LLM polishing or rewriting preserves core content while making style more uniform, "reducing writing-complexity variance by a statistically significant 21-50% across datasets and models" — [Sourati et al., arXiv 2502.11266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11266). HIGH (abstract; the arXiv page lists Nature Human Behaviour 2026 while a search summary says 2025, so the venue year is unresolved).
- Semantic drift under narrow instruction: on 2021 human essays revised by an LLM with expert feedback and an instruction to make only grammar edits, "they still change the text in a way that significantly alters its semantic meaning" (Abdulhai et al.) — [arXiv 2603.18161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161). HIGH (abstract; sample sizes not on the page).
- Human-like editing: humans make self-contained, meaning-preserving edits while LLMs often make scattered edits that change meaning; sentence-level edit suggestions that can be accepted or rejected independently, trained with a reward over edit-level semantic similarity, fluency and pattern conformity, outperform baselines in human evaluation (Ziegenbein et al., April 2026) — [arXiv 2604.12770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12770). HIGH (abstract; the multi-round fluency and meaning-drift claim in a search summary was not on this page, so excluded).
- Same-model scoring vs humans over iterations: Pan et al. found evaluator ratings rise while human-judged essay quality stalls or falls, worse when generator and evaluator share context — [arXiv 2407.04549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549). HIGH (abstract; cited from earlier notes, not re-derived).
- Self-Refine code optimization gains were small (+3.6 to +8.7) compared to preference-style tasks — cited from earlier notes, [Self-Refine full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Multi-axis trade-off in Constitutional AI revisions: harmlessness up, helpfulness down — [arXiv 2212.08073 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08073). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Evaluator exploitation: see Sakana CUDA Engineer in Section 1 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2969225), [EvoEngineer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03760). MEDIUM.

### Inferences
- Real, human-visible gains at high quality come from loops where the metric is the thing the user cares about and is hardened against exploitation (speed, size, test coverage, benchmark scores). For prose, UI and plans the evidence supports small, early, targeted improvements and documents harm from open-ended "make it better" iteration. MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Style flattening (21-50% less variance) and meaning shift under grammar-only instructions are measured at corpus level on lower-bar text; whether a single polish of an already-excellent, voice-rich piece loses as much is not measured. The risk is still concrete enough to require an explicit voice-and-meaning invariant check. MEDIUM.
- Axis trade-offs (feasibility falls as originality rises; helpfulness falls as harmlessness rises; pragmatism falls as speed rises) mean "better" must be accepted per axis with non-regression on untargeted axes. MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Claude 3.5 Sonnet's higher correctness but lower human ratings in the writing testbed shows correctness-style metrics and human preference can diverge at the top end for edits; the harm channel there was volume of insertion. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found of human-judged improvement from a single "polish" pass on an already-excellent human-written text; the closest evidence is corpus-level flattening and writing-agent ratings from 2024 models.
- No peer-reviewed measurement of "quality drift after repeated rewrites" by current frontier models on strong prose; Javaji et al. use mixed model sizes and vague prompts by design.
- Objective-metric gains on code speed, size and coverage from agentic loops were not independently benchmarked beyond AlphaEvolve's vendor-reported results and the Sakana/KernelBench episode (secondary sources).
- The Beel et al. AI Scientist evaluation is unverified at page level (search summary only).

## 5. Subtractive versus additive refinement

### Takeaway
LLMs have a measured addition bias that gets worse under "improve" framing, deletion is a documented weak skill in code editing, and in a writing-edit study larger deletions raised and larger insertions lowered human ratings. No controlled study compares removal-first against addition-first refinement passes on quality directly, so the case for subtraction-first is convergent but indirect.

### Cited Findings
- Addition bias (Santagata and De Nobili): across GPT-3.5 Turbo, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Mistral, MathΣtral and Llama 3.1, a clear preference for additive changes; Llama 3.1 favored adding letters 97.85% of the time in a palindrome task; GPT-3.5 Turbo added a Lego brick 76.38% of the time rather than removing one; Mistral 7B produced longer summaries in 59.40% to 75.10% of cases when asked to improve its own or others' writing — [arXiv 2409.02569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02569). HIGH (abstract; preprint with no peer-reviewed venue listed; small controlled tasks).
- Uhler et al. (4 preregistered experiments, 588 humans and 680 GPT-4 iterations): GPT-4 showed a stronger tendency toward additive transformations than humans; humans used additive strategies less when subtraction was more efficient, GPT-4 showed "a strong addition bias when subtraction was more efficient"; GPT-4 used additive strategies more when told to "improve" (positive valence) than to "edit" (neutral) — [arXiv 2404.16692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16692) (the page lists publication in Communications Psychology, 2026). HIGH (abstract level; GPT-4).
- Deletion avoidance in code (CanItDelete, Ebrahimi et al., July 2026): 200 tasks mined from real commits where the required edit contains no added code; even on tasks all five top models solve on SWE-bench Verified, deletion recall against the developer patch reaches at most 71.7%; models find the right file for over 92% of required deletions but edit the exact line in under 52%; 29.0% of passing patches wrap the targeted code in a guard or fallback ("Guard-and-Go"); on 34 Verified tasks retrofitted with tests that fail if the targeted code remains, four frontier models dropped from 63.2% to 41.9%; prompt changes alone moved success little; a pilot showed post-training on deletion reduced the avoidance — [arXiv 2607.28887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28887). HIGH for the abstract-level claims, MEDIUM for numbers (tool-extracted; very recent preprint, not independently replicated; the tool named a "GPT-5.6 Sol" model which I could not verify).
- Writing-agent study: "Larger deletions correlated with higher ratings (p < 0.001)" and larger insertions with lower ratings — [arXiv 2503.19711 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19711v1). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Length inflation under vague iteration: Claude over 40x and GPT-OSS-20B 34x code length — [arXiv 2509.06770 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06770). HIGH (tool-extracted).
- Judge verbosity preference makes additive edits look better to a judge than they are: verbosity prompts swing raw AlpacaEval win rates from 22.9% to 64.3% — [length-control paper listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04475) (MEDIUM, search-summary figures); GPT-3.5 and Claude-v1 fell for a repetitive-list verbosity attack in 91.3% of cases — [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685) (HIGH).
- Subtractive method at the system level: Anthropic's harness author removed one component at a time and measured the effect, finding the sprint construct, per-sprint grading and context resets unnecessary for the newer model, while keeping the planner and the end-of-run evaluator — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps). HIGH (tool-extracted; one author's experience).
- Autoreason's word-count drop of 59-70% over 15 passes on Haiku 3.5, with judged quality winning ("21/28 wins" in length-controlled evaluation against baselines) — [NousResearch/autoreason README](https://github.com/NousResearch/autoreason). LOW (unreviewed repository).
- AlphaEvolve's use of LLM-scored "program simplicity" as a criterion inside the score dictionary, or to discard candidates failing a criterion — [arXiv 2506.13131 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131v1). HIGH (tool-extracted).

### Inferences
- The default direction of an LLM "improvement" pass is additive, the judge is biased toward added length, and humans rate insertions lower and deletions higher in at least one writing-edit study. A pass that does not impose a subtractive bias will, by default, grow the artifact. MEDIUM-HIGH (four convergent HIGH sources, each indirect).
- Because the word "improve" increases additive behavior in GPT-4 (Uhler), a skill whose framing is "make it perfect" risks inheriting the effect; neutral edit framing plus an explicit subtraction-first checklist is the testable mitigation. MEDIUM (one study, GPT-4 only).
- Deletion needs explicit scaffolding: even correct localization fails at the exact-line step (<52%) and prompt changes alone help little (CanItDelete), so the skill should name candidates for removal itself and verify removal with a check that fails if the removed thing still exists. MEDIUM.
- Subtraction is not automatically safe: deletions must be verified against behavior (tests) in code and against meaning and voice in prose. MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment comparing subtraction-first and addition-first refinement for human-judged quality of LLM output was found; the claim "removal passes improve quality more reliably than addition" is not directly supported, only that additive default behavior is common and associated with lower ratings and higher length.
- The writing-agent result is one 2025 preprint on 2024 models; the correlation between deletions and ratings is not causal.
- Human-craft evidence on cutting is another researcher's scope and not covered here.

## 6. Design rules for a good-to-perfect pass, with falsification conditions

### Takeaway
Treat the finished result as an incumbent; raise the bar through contrast with stronger exemplars and a versioned rubric rather than self-assessment; accept a change only through objective checks first and a blind, order-swapped, length-normalized, multi-family comparison second, with ties going to the incumbent; make subtraction the default move; bound the loop at a few cycles and stop at a fixed point, never at a claim of perfection.

### Cited Findings
- Rules below are inferences; their supporting sources are cited inline and in Sections 1-5 and the earlier notes at `/Users/firegroup/projects/claude-plugins/research_notes/AI post task bar raising skill/self_refinement_evidence.md`.

### Inferences
Each rule lists supporting sources, a confidence label, and the observation that would falsify it. Positioning against `/Users/firegroup/projects/claude-plugins/plugins/aio-code-review/skills/aio-code-review/SKILL.md:54`: the evidence supports the "better, not perfect" stance in that perfection cannot be verified absolutely (criteria drift, judge ceilings); it supports a good-to-perfect skill only if "perfect" is defined as a verified fixed point under an explicit, rising, logged bar, not as an assertion.

1. **Incumbent holds unless a challenger wins a gated comparison; keep the best, never the last.** Snapshot the finished result; every candidate change competes against that snapshot; the final output is the best-scoring state, with "no change" a first-class result. Support: Self-Refine best-of-iterations (earlier notes), [Snell et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03314) (about 38% correct-to-incorrect flips without selection), [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) (middle iteration preferred over last), [Javaji et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06770) (90% to near 0% passing in four turns), [Autoreason](https://github.com/NousResearch/autoreason) (LOW). Confidence MEDIUM-HIGH for keep-best, MEDIUM for the exact incumbent protocol. Falsified if, over a set of strong starting artifacts, last-iteration outputs are judged by blind humans at least as good as best-selected outputs.

2. **Objective checks accept or reject first; judges only decide what checks cannot.** Run whatever is machine-checkable (tests, build, type check, benchmark, size, coverage, lint, link check) as a cascade, cheapest first; require non-regression on all pre-existing checks plus held-out checks the editing step did not see; use an LLM judge only for the residual subjective axes. Support: [FunSearch](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/funsearch-making-new-discoveries-in-mathematical-sciences-using-large-language-models/), [AlphaEvolve evaluator cascade](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131v1), [Mind Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09891), [Large Language Monkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787), [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760), Sakana CUDA ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2969225), [EvoEngineer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03760), MEDIUM), [Autoreason](https://github.com/NousResearch/autoreason) (gains vanish on held-out tests, LOW). Confidence HIGH for objective-first, MEDIUM for the held-out requirement. Falsified if a judge-only gate matches an objective-plus-held-out gate on regression rate on a coding or measurable task set.

3. **For subjective axes use a judge protocol sized to small differences: both orders, inconsistent equals tie, at least two model families or a fresh-context reasoning judge, pairwise plus pointwise rubric verdicts agreeing, length-normalized; a tie keeps the incumbent.** Support: [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685) (66% with ties vs 85% without; position consistency 65% for GPT-4), [Shi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v9) (bias grows as quality gap shrinks; majority of capable judges), [Tripathi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14716) (pairwise flips 35% vs absolute 9% under distractors), [PoLL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796), [JudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12784) (hard pairs near chance for non-reasoning judges), [AlpacaEval length control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475). Confidence MEDIUM (components HIGH, combination untested). Falsified if a single-judge, single-order verdict agrees with blind human preference on near-tied candidate pairs as well as the multi-protocol verdict.

4. **Calibrate the protocol's resolution before trusting it: seed known-better and known-worse pairs from the artifact itself and require the judge to rank them correctly at a stated rate.** If the judge cannot separate a deliberately degraded copy from the incumbent, it cannot certify a subtle improvement, and the pass reports "not verifiable at this resolution". Support: [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) (few-shot calibration, reading evaluator logs, evaluator leniency toward LLM output), [JudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784), [Arena-Hard-Auto separability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11939), [Anthropic multi-agent system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) (humans caught what automated evals missed). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if judges that fail the seeded-pair check still agree with blind human rankings on real candidate pairs at the same rate as judges that pass.

5. **The bar rises by contrast, not by self-assertion: derive new rubric items from the gap between the incumbent and a stronger exemplar (or a stronger sibling variant), keep only items that separate them, add them to a versioned rubric, and re-judge.** Keep a frozen core taken from the user's request that new items may not override. Support: [OpenRubrics/CRG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07743) (+8.4% over size-matched baselines), [SibylSense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20751) (static rubrics decay and are exploited), [Shankar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12272) (criteria drift; cannot fix criteria before seeing outputs), [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) (criteria wording steers output; weight the axes the model is weak on), [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07506) (judges ignore references that conflict with their own knowledge). Confidence MEDIUM-LOW (no direct test of exemplar-conditioned critique specificity). Falsified if rubrics extended with exemplar-contrast items produce no more human-confirmed improvements per cycle than the original rubric alone, or if escalated rubrics make accepted changes drift away from the user's stated requirements.

6. **Default to subtraction: the first candidate moves are cut, merge, simplify and delete; additions must pass a stricter gate (a named gap against the rubric, objective check or exemplar contrast) and a size budget.** Prefer neutral edit framing over "improve" or "perfect". Support: [Santagata and De Nobili](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02569), [Uhler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16692) ("improve" raises additive behavior), [CanItDelete](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28887), [Gooding et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19711v1) (deletions up, insertions down in ratings), [Javaji et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06770) (length inflation), [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685) and [AlpacaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475) (judges reward length). Confidence MEDIUM (convergent, indirect). Falsified if, on matched artifacts, subtraction-first passes yield lower blind-human preference than addition-first passes, or if size budgets reduce measured quality on tasks that genuinely need more content.

7. **Lock identity and scope: declare invariants (author voice, meaning, public interface, scope) before editing, make changes as small independently acceptable edits, and reject any edit that shifts them.** Check voice and meaning with a comparison the judge did not write; list out-of-scope ideas instead of applying them. Support: [Sourati et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11266) (21-50% style-variance reduction under rewriting), [Abdulhai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161) (meaning shifts even under grammar-only instruction), [Ziegenbein et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12770) (accept or reject sentence-level edits independently), [Gooding et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19711v1) (semantic drift lowers ratings), earlier notes (scope lock, rule 9). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if whole-document rewrites score equal or better than minimal edit sets in blind human preference without more voice or meaning loss on strong inputs.

8. **Accept per axis, not on a single score: require non-regression on every axis not being targeted, and report any trade made.** Support: [Constitutional AI](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08073) (harmlessness up, helpfulness down; preference-model calibration degrades at high scores), [Javaji et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06770) (feasibility fell as originality rose; pragmatism fell as performance prompts rose), [Gooding et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19711v1) (correctness without user preference). Confidence MEDIUM-HIGH. Falsified if single-score acceptance produces the same untargeted-axis regressions as per-axis acceptance on a multi-axis task set.

9. **Use N independent challengers only when the gate is discriminative; otherwise edit sequentially with small steps.** With an objective evaluator, sample a few challengers from the incumbent in fresh contexts and select by the gate; without one, keep N small (2 to 3), because more candidates mostly yield more false "improvements" under a noisy judge. Support: [Snell et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03314) (sequential wins on easy problems, mixed on hard; allocation by difficulty), [Mind Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09891), [Large Language Monkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787) (selection without verifiers plateaus), [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760), Contextual Drag (earlier notes; fresh context per challenger). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if best-of-N with an LLM-only judge improves blind-human-rated quality monotonically as N grows on strong inputs.

10. **Cap the loop: default two accepted-or-rejected cycles, hard stop at three; stop early after two consecutive cycles where no challenger beats the incumbent; restart from the incumbent plus the logged findings in clean context after any rejected cycle that polluted the context.** Support: CRITIC 2-3 rounds and Self-Refine diminishing returns (earlier notes), [Javaji et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06770) (gains early, collapse after vague turns), [Autoreason k=2](https://github.com/NousResearch/autoreason) (LOW), [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) (5-15 iterations with plateau and non-monotonic path, but at about 20x cost on tasks beyond the solo model). Confidence MEDIUM for the cap, HIGH for diminishing returns. Falsified if with a hardened objective evaluator quality still rises materially in cycles 4 and beyond at acceptable cost.

11. **Match effort to headroom: skip or shrink the pass where the model already does the task reliably or where nothing checkable remains; spend on tasks at the edge of ability or with a measurable target.** Support: [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) ("worth the cost when the task sits beyond what the current model does reliably solo"; evaluator unnecessary for tasks Opus 4.6 handled alone), [Olausson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896) (repair gains modest after cost; earlier notes), [Anthropic multi-agent system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) (effect sizes large early, so small samples suffice early; the reverse holds near the top, where effects are small and need larger or objective evals). Confidence MEDIUM. Falsified if an always-on full pass shows better cost-adjusted outcomes than a headroom-gated pass on a mixed task set.

12. **Define the stop condition as a verified fixed point, and report it as one: "converged under rubric vN with checks C; K challengers tried, M accepted, reasons for rejections; remaining known gaps", never "perfect".** Escalate to the user when the remaining differences are taste-level or when the judge fails the resolution check in rule 4. Support: [Shankar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12272) (criteria cannot be fully fixed in advance), [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) (scores plateau with headroom remaining), [JudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784) and [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685) (judge ceilings), `aio-code-review/SKILL.md:54`. Confidence MEDIUM (design choice anchored to HIGH evidence on unverifiability). Falsified if users given the fixed-point report make equal or worse follow-up edits than users given a "no further improvements possible" claim, or if reports of fixed points are routinely overturned by a stronger judge or a human on the next review.

### Gaps
- None of the 12 rules was tested end-to-end as a Claude Code skill; the quickest falsification is an A/B on strong artifacts (code with benchmarks, prose with blind human preference, plans, UI) comparing: no pass, edit-in-place without gate, and the gated incumbent/challenger pass, scored by blind humans plus objective checks, including an over-polish negative control (an already-excellent artifact that should come back unchanged).
- Thresholds (two cycles, cap three, N of 2 to 3, K=2) are design choices anchored to Self-Refine/CRITIC curves and Autoreason, not measured for Claude Code.
- Rules 4 and 5 (resolution calibration and exemplar-contrast escalation) are the least supported by direct evidence and are the central new mechanisms the skill adds relative to the earlier "bar is met" design.
- Judge-family diversity (rules 3 and 4) depends on what models the skill's users can run; the marketplace skill may have only same-model subagents, in which case rule 3's family requirement reduces to fresh-context plus reasoning-mode judging, which is weaker (self-preference, AI-AI bias).
