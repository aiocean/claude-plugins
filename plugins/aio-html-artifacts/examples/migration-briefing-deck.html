Objection one · capacity

Peak load is not the problem.

Peak consumer utilisation at 2.4× expected traffic, on both designs. The autoscale trigger sits at 80%, and 30 minutes of steady state left 25 points of headroom under it.

Return here whenever throughput comes back. The two designs are within 0.8 points of each other; capacity is not what separates them.

Mechanism · what differs

A failure starts two clocks.

Legacy — the broker owns the clock

  1. A worker dies mid-message.
  2. The broker keeps it invisible until the visibility timeout expires: 5m00s.
  3. Another consumer sees the work only after that.

Target — the application owns the clock

  1. A worker dies mid-message.
  2. Its retry intent is already durable, written before the handler.
  3. The scheduler picks that intent up on its own interval: 30s.

The timeout, not consumer capacity, sets the floor under legacy recovery — very likely (80-95%).

The 5m00s number is the broker default nobody has changed. Naming who owns the clock is the whole argument of the deck.

Sequence · reversible while it runs

Migrate the clock before the traffic.

  1. 01Shadow the intent. Legacy stays authoritative. Rollback: stop writing.
  2. 02Compare recovery for a week. Count missing, duplicate and late outcomes on both clocks. Rollback: none, this step only reads.
  3. 03Move one cohort, 10% of traffic. Rollback: one config flag, fired automatically when parity drops.
  4. 04Retire the timeout. Rollback: none. The one-way step is last on purpose.

Run the steps in this order: every step before the last one reverses with a flag.

Step 2 produces the parity report. That report, not this deck, decides the cutover.

Refutation

What would show this is wrong.

  • Re-run CH-77 on the target path with the scheduler interval raised to 5m. If p95 recovery stays under 120 s, the clock is not what moves recovery and this briefing fails.
  • Read the broker config as it stood on 2026-08-12. A timeout already under 60 s puts another cause behind 7m 04s, and scene 5 buys nothing.

I assumed the broker keeps its 5m00s visibility timeout through the migration window. The staging config CH-77 ran on carries that value; the production broker config is unread.

Offer the first falsifier as a 40-minute job. Naming the cheap refutation is what turns a briefing into a decision.
