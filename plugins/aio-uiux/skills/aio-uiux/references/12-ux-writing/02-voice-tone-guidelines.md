# Voice and Tone Guidelines

Voice is who you are. Tone is how you sound right now. A brand has one voice — confident, warm, direct — but its tone shifts with context. The same person who speaks warmly at a party speaks differently at a funeral. That is not inconsistency. That is emotional intelligence.

---

## 1. Voice vs. Tone

**Voice** = stable brand personality. Defined once. Applied everywhere.
**Tone** = emotional register adapted to context. Changes per situation.

| | Voice | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Stays the same | Yes | No |
| Defined by | Brand values | User's emotional state |
| Example | Direct | Urgent (error) vs. Celebratory (achievement) |

### Voice Attributes — How to Define Them

A voice is not a list of adjectives. It is a pair of contrasts: what you are, and what you are not. This prevents drift.

**Format: We are [X], not [Y]**

GOOD voice definition:
- We are **direct**, not blunt
- We are **warm**, not gushing
- We are **expert**, not condescending
- We are **playful**, not silly
- We are **honest**, not harsh

BAD voice definition (too vague to be actionable):
- We are friendly
- We are professional
- We are helpful

## 2. Content Principles

### Clarity Over Cleverness

Users come to get things done, not to be impressed. If a sentence is clever but confusing, it failed. Plain language is always the right call.

BAD: `Leverage synergistic touchpoints to maximize your operational throughput.`
GOOD: `Use your team's tools together to get more done.`

BAD: `Harness the power of AI to supercharge your workflow.`
GOOD: `Let AI handle the repetitive work so you can focus on what matters.`

BAD: `We've sunset this feature.`
GOOD: `This feature is no longer available.`

BAD: `Your journey starts here.`
GOOD: `Create your account to get started.`

### Concise

Cut every word that does not carry meaning. Longer does not mean more professional. It means harder to read.

BAD: `In order to be able to export your data, you will need to first navigate to the Settings page.`
GOOD: `To export your data, go to Settings.`

BAD: `Please be advised that your account password has been successfully updated.`
GOOD: `Password updated.`

BAD: `We wanted to reach out to let you know that your subscription is coming up for renewal.`
GOOD: `Your subscription renews on March 15.`

BAD: `At this point in time, the feature is not available.`
GOOD: `This feature isn't available yet.`

### Actionable

Tell users what to do, not just what is happening. Every message is an opportunity to guide.

BAD: `Subscription expired.`
GOOD: `Your subscription expired on Jan 3. Renew now to restore access.`

BAD: `You are not connected.`
GOOD: `You're offline. Check your connection and try again.`

BAD: `File is too large.`
GOOD: `File exceeds 10 MB. Compress it or upload a smaller version.`

### Consistent

Use the same term for the same thing everywhere. Variation breeds confusion.

BAD: Using "workspace," "account," and "organization" interchangeably.
GOOD: Pick one term and use it throughout (e.g., "workspace").

BAD: "Sign in" on the homepage, "Log in" in the app, "Login" in emails.
GOOD: "Sign in" everywhere.

BAD: "Delete," "Remove," and "Archive" used for the same action.
GOOD: Use "Delete" for permanent removal, "Archive" for hiding but keeping, "Remove" for detaching from a group.

## 3. Tone by Emotional Context

### Celebration / Achievement

The user completed something meaningful. Match their energy — briefly. Don't be over the top.

**Tone**: Warm, specific, brief. Acknowledge the achievement, then move them forward.

BAD: `Success!!!!`
BAD: `You did it! Congratulations! Amazing work! You're incredible!`
GOOD: `You launched your first campaign. 🎉`
GOOD: `Project created. Here's what to do next.`
GOOD: `All 47 items exported successfully.`

**Do not**: Use excessive exclamation marks. Do not celebrate routine actions (saving a file is not a party).

### Error / Failure

The user hit a wall. They may be frustrated. Do not add to the frustration with blame, jargon, or vague language.

**Tone**: Calm, clear, helpful. Acknowledge the problem. Give a path forward.

BAD: `Oops! Something went wrong!`
BAD: `Error 403: Forbidden access to requested resource.`
BAD: `Your session has been invalidated due to inactivity parameters.`
GOOD: `We couldn't save your changes. Check your connection and try again.`
GOOD: `You don't have permission to view this page. Ask your admin for access.`
GOOD: `Your session expired. Sign in again to continue — your work is saved.`

**Do not**: Use "Oops" or casual apologies for serious errors. Do not use technical codes without explanation.

### Routine / Neutral

The user is in flow. Don't interrupt with personality. Be invisible.

**Tone**: Efficient, plain, zero friction.

BAD: `Fantastic! Your message has been sent successfully!`
GOOD: `Message sent.`

BAD: `We're so glad you're here! Let's get you set up!`
GOOD: `Set up your account.`

BAD: `Wonderful! Your file has been uploaded with flying colors!`
GOOD: `File uploaded.`

### Warning / Caution

The user is about to do something with consequences, or something may go wrong. Inform without alarming.

**Tone**: Clear, measured, factual. State the risk and give a choice.

BAD: `WARNING: CRITICAL ACTION REQUIRED IMMEDIATELY`
BAD: `Be careful! You might lose your data!`
GOOD: `You have unsaved changes. Leave without saving?`
GOOD: `This will affect all 340 users in your workspace.`
GOOD: `Free plan limit reached. Upgrade to continue uploading files.`

### Empathy / Support

The user is struggling, confused, or frustrated. Meet them where they are.

**Tone**: Human, patient, non-judgmental.

BAD: `Your payment failed. Update your billing information.`
GOOD: `Your payment didn't go through. This can happen for a few reasons — check that your card details are up to date or try a different card.`

BAD: `Account locked due to multiple failed login attempts.`
GOOD: `Your account has been temporarily locked after several failed attempts. This protects your account. Reset your password to unlock it.`

## 4. Word Lists

### Preferred Terms vs. Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Utilize | Use | Simpler |
| Leverage | Use, apply, tap into | Jargon |
| Synergy | (rewrite the sentence) | Meaningless |
| Best-in-class | (use specifics) | Unsubstantiated |
| Seamless | Smooth, easy, simple | Overused |
| Robust | Powerful, reliable, thorough | Vague |
| Intuitive | Easy to use | Unverifiable |
| Cutting-edge | Advanced, new, latest | Overused |
| Innovative | (describe the innovation) | Vague |
| Scalable | Grows with you | Jargon |
| Ecosystem | Platform, tools, products | Jargon |
| Onboard | Set up, get started | Can use either |
| Sunset | Remove, discontinue, end | Euphemistic |
| Spin up | Create, start, launch | Jargon |
| Surface (verb) | Show, display, reveal | Jargon |

### Action Verbs — Use These

- Start (not begin, initiate, commence)
- Create (not generate, produce, spin up)
- Delete (not remove, erase — unless you mean detach)
- Send (not dispatch, submit in most cases)
- Save (not persist, commit — unless developer context)
- Sign in / Sign out (not log in / log out — pick one, be consistent)
- Cancel (not abort)
- Update (not modify, alter)
- Connect (not integrate, in most user-facing contexts)

## 5. Capitalization Rules

### Sentence Case (Preferred for UI)

Use sentence case for: button labels, form labels, error messages, tooltips, empty states, toast messages, modal titles, menu items.

**Sentence case**: Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only.

GOOD: `Create new project`
GOOD: `Save changes`
GOOD: `Delete this workspace`
GOOD: `Email address`

BAD: `Create New Project`
BAD: `Save Changes`
BAD: `Delete This Workspace`
BAD: `Email Address`

### Title Case

Reserve title case for: page headings, major section headers, document titles, navigation items (product-specific).

GOOD page title: `Account Settings`
GOOD nav item: `Team Members`
GOOD doc title: `Getting Started Guide`

### ALL CAPS

Avoid. It reads as shouting and is harder to read. Exception: abbreviations (API, URL, FAQ).

BAD: `DELETE ACCOUNT`
BAD: `WARNING: IRREVERSIBLE ACTION`
GOOD: `Delete account`
GOOD: `This action cannot be undone.`

## 6. Punctuation Rules

### Periods

- Use in full sentences in body copy, help text, and error messages.
- Omit at the end of button labels, form labels, and single-line UI strings.
- Omit in toast messages (they are brief and glanceable).

GOOD body text: `Your password must be at least 8 characters.`
GOOD button: `Save changes` (no period)
GOOD label: `Email address` (no period)
GOOD toast: `Changes saved` (no period)

### Exclamation Marks

Use sparingly. Maximum once per screen, and only for genuine moments of delight or achievement. Never in errors.

BAD: `Error saving file!`
BAD: `Welcome! Sign in to get started! Your account is ready!`
GOOD: `You're all set!` (first-run completion, once)

### Ellipses

Use to indicate in-progress actions. Do not use to trail off in labels.

GOOD: `Saving…` (action in progress)
GOOD: `Loading your dashboard…`
BAD: `Not sure what to add…` (trailing off in help text)
BAD: `You can add more later…` (implies uncertainty)

### Apostrophes and Contractions

Use contractions in conversational copy. They reduce reading friction and sound more human.

GOOD: `You don't have any projects yet.`
GOOD: `We couldn't save your changes.`
GOOD: `You're all set.`
BAD: `You do not have any projects yet.`
BAD: `We could not save your changes.`

Exception: Legal text, terms, formal notices — avoid contractions.

### Oxford Comma

Use it. Always. It prevents ambiguity.

BAD: `Export to PDF, CSV and Excel`
GOOD: `Export to PDF, CSV, and Excel`

## 7. Reading Level

Target Grade 8 (Flesch-Kincaid). This is not about writing for low literacy — it is about writing efficiently. Grade 8 means sentences average 15–20 words, common vocabulary, active voice.

### Active vs. Passive Voice

Active voice is shorter, clearer, and more direct.

BAD (passive): `Your account has been deleted by an administrator.`
GOOD (active): `An administrator deleted your account.`

BAD (passive): `The file could not be uploaded due to a size limit being exceeded.`
GOOD (active): `The file is too large to upload. Maximum size is 10 MB.`

BAD (passive): `Changes will be applied after the page has been refreshed.`
GOOD (active): `Refresh the page to apply changes.`

### Sentence Length

Keep sentences under 20 words where possible. If a sentence needs a semicolon or more than one comma, split it.

BAD: `In order to complete your profile, which is required before you can invite team members, please upload a photo and add your job title and department.`

GOOD: `Complete your profile before inviting team members. Add a photo, your job title, and your department.`

## 8. Brand Voice in Practice

### Scenario: New user signs up

BAD (corporate): `Welcome to the platform. Your account registration has been successfully completed. Please proceed to configure your settings.`

BAD (try-hard friendly): `OMG you're here! We're SO excited to have you! Let's get this party started! 🎉🎉🎉`

GOOD (warm + direct): `Welcome to Acme. You're all set — let's get your workspace ready in about 3 minutes.`

### Scenario: Feature not available on free plan

BAD (aggressive upsell): `UPGRADE NOW to unlock this POWERFUL feature!`

BAD (apologetic): `We're so sorry, but unfortunately this feature is only available on paid plans.`

GOOD (honest + value-focused): `Custom domains are available on the Pro plan. Upgrade to connect your own domain and remove Acme branding.`

### Scenario: App maintenance

BAD: `503 Service Unavailable`

BAD: `Oops! Our servers are having issues. We're working on it!`

GOOD: `We're doing scheduled maintenance until 3:00 PM UTC. Your data is safe. We'll be back shortly.`
