# Conversion Copy

Conversion copy moves people from interest to action. It applies to landing pages, pricing pages, signup flows, upgrade prompts, and any moment where a user decides whether to commit. Good conversion copy removes friction and earns trust. Bad conversion copy applies pressure and erodes it.

---

## 1. Headlines That Convert

Headlines are read by 5x more people than body copy. They have one job: make the next line irresistible to read.

### Principles
- Lead with the outcome the user wants, not the feature you built
- Be specific — numbers and named outcomes outperform vague claims
- Write for the reader's situation, not your product's capabilities
- The best headlines answer: "What will this do for me, specifically?"

### Benefit-Driven Headlines

**Pattern: [User goal] without [obstacle]**

BAD: `The all-in-one project management solution for modern teams`
GOOD: `Ship projects on time without drowning in status meetings`

BAD: `AI-powered writing assistant`
GOOD: `Write twice as fast. Sound twice as good.`

BAD: `Advanced analytics platform`
GOOD: `Know which campaigns made money — before your next budget meeting`

**Pattern: Specific outcome + timeframe**

BAD: `Grow your business`
GOOD: `Get your first 100 customers in 30 days`

BAD: `Save time on reporting`
GOOD: `Cut reporting time from 4 hours to 20 minutes`

BAD: `Better team collaboration`
GOOD: `Your team's work, in one place, updated in real time`

### Numbered Headlines

Numbers add specificity and set expectations. They work because they are concrete and scannable.

BAD: `Many ways to customize your dashboard`
GOOD: `12 ways to customize your dashboard — no code required`

BAD: `Dozens of integrations available`
GOOD: `Connect to 90+ tools you already use`

BAD: `Reduce your workload`
GOOD: `Automate 6 hours of weekly admin work`

### Sub-Headlines

The sub-headline supports the main headline by adding a second benefit or addressing the main objection.

BAD sub-headline: `The best tool for your team`
GOOD sub-headline: `Used by 50,000 teams at companies like Stripe, Notion, and Figma`

BAD sub-headline: `Sign up today!`
GOOD sub-headline: `Free for 14 days. No credit card required.`

BAD sub-headline: `Powerful and easy to use`
GOOD sub-headline: `Set up in 10 minutes. Cancel anytime.`

## 2. CTA Optimization

CTAs are the most tested element in conversion copy. Small changes in CTA text produce measurable differences in click-through rate.

### The Formula: Verb + Value

Every CTA should complete the sentence: "I want to ___"

BAD: `Submit`
GOOD: `Start free trial`

BAD: `Click here`
GOOD: `Get my free report`

BAD: `Sign up`
GOOD: `Create my free account`

BAD: `Learn more`
GOOD: `See how it works` (for video) or `Read the full guide` (for article)

BAD: `Buy now`
GOOD: `Get started for $29/month`

### First-Person CTAs

First-person CTAs ("my" instead of "your") consistently outperform second-person in testing.

BAD: `Start your free trial`
GOOD: `Start my free trial`

BAD: `Get your free report`
GOOD: `Get my free report`

BAD: `Claim your discount`
GOOD: `Claim my 20% discount`

### CTA Supporting Copy

The micro-copy beneath a CTA button can remove objections before the user forms them.

BAD: Primary CTA button with no supporting text.

GOOD:
```
[Start free trial]
14 days free. No credit card. Cancel anytime.
```

GOOD:
```
[Get started free]
Join 50,000 teams. Setup takes 5 minutes.
```

GOOD:
```
[Upgrade to Pro]
Billed monthly. Cancel or downgrade anytime.
```

### CTA Button Text Length

Keep it under 5 words where possible. The button should be readable at a glance.

BAD: `Click here to start your free 14-day trial today`
GOOD: `Start free trial`

BAD: `Download the complete guide for free now`
GOOD: `Download free guide`

## 3. Social Proof

Social proof reduces the perceived risk of a decision. It works because humans look to others when uncertain. Use it close to the CTA, not buried on a separate page.

### Types of Social Proof (in order of trust value)

1. Named customer testimonials with photo + title + company
2. Case study results (specific numbers, named company)
3. Customer logos (recognizable brands)
4. Star ratings and review counts
5. User count / signup count
6. Press mentions

### Testimonial Copy Patterns

BAD testimonial:
> "This product is great! Highly recommend!"
> — John S.

GOOD testimonial:
> "We cut our weekly reporting time from 6 hours to 45 minutes. Our VP now gets the data she needs without asking for it."
> — Sarah Chen, Head of Marketing, Shopify

**What makes it good:** Specific outcome (6 hours → 45 minutes), specific role (Head of Marketing), recognizable company (Shopify), named person.

BAD testimonial placement: On a "Testimonials" page nobody navigates to.
GOOD testimonial placement: Directly beside or below the primary CTA.

### Review Count Formatting

BAD: `Hundreds of happy customers`
GOOD: `4.9/5 from 2,340 reviews on G2`

BAD: `Trusted by teams everywhere`
GOOD: `Used by 50,000+ teams in 140 countries`

BAD: `Industry leading`
GOOD: `#1 rated project tool on G2 and Capterra`

### Case Study Snippets

BAD: `Acme helped Company X improve their processes.`
GOOD: `Company X reduced customer onboarding time by 60% using Acme's automated workflows.` with [Read case study] link

## 4. Pricing Page Patterns

Pricing pages are where conversion either happens or collapses. The copy must answer: "Is this worth it for me?"

### Plan Naming

Avoid arbitrary tier names that require decoding. Use names that signal who each plan is for.

BAD: `Starter / Professional / Enterprise`
GOOD: `For individuals / For teams / For companies`

BAD: `Basic / Plus / Pro`
GOOD: `Solo / Team / Business` (if target segments are clear)

Alternative: Keep "Starter / Pro / Enterprise" but add a sub-label:
GOOD:
```
Starter
For freelancers and solo operators

Pro
For growing teams of 2–20

Enterprise
For large teams with compliance needs
```

### "Most Popular" Badging

Use a "Most popular" badge on the plan you want to sell most. Place it on the plan with the best value-to-price ratio, not the most expensive.

BAD: Badging the most expensive plan as "Most popular" (users distrust this).
GOOD: Badge the mid-tier plan that genuinely has the best feature-to-price ratio.

### Feature List Copy

Describe features from the user's perspective. What do they get to do?

BAD:
- Unlimited projects
- API access
- 25 GB storage
- SSO

GOOD:
- Unlimited projects — never hit a cap
- API access — connect to any tool in your stack
- 25 GB storage — store all your assets in one place
- SSO — sign in with your company credentials

### Pricing Page CTAs

Each plan needs its own CTA that matches what the plan offers.

BAD: All three plans say `Get started`
GOOD:
- Free plan: `Start for free — no card needed`
- Pro plan: `Start 14-day free trial`
- Enterprise plan: `Talk to sales`

### Annual vs. Monthly Toggle

When showing annual pricing, make the saving visible and specific.

BAD: `Annual billing: $99/year`
GOOD: `Annual billing: $8.25/month — save $21 vs. monthly`

BAD: Just a toggle with no explanation of savings.
GOOD: `Save 20% with annual billing` badge next to the toggle, with per-month equivalent shown.

## 5. Signup Flow Friction Reduction

Every field in a signup form is a reason to abandon. Reduce fields to the absolute minimum required to deliver value.

### Field Count

BAD: First name, last name, email, phone, company, company size, role, how did you hear about us, password, confirm password, agree to terms (11 fields)

GOOD: Email, password, agree to terms (3 fields) — everything else collected progressively after signup

### Social Login

Always offer social login (Google, GitHub, Apple) above the email form. It removes password friction entirely.

GOOD:
```
[Continue with Google]
[Continue with GitHub]

──── or ────

Email
Password
[Create account]
```

BAD: Email form only, with social login as a small link at the bottom.

### Form Headline Copy

BAD: `Create an account`
GOOD: `Start your free trial — no card required`

BAD: `Register`
GOOD: `Get started in 2 minutes`

BAD: `Sign up for Acme`
GOOD: `Join 50,000 teams on Acme`

### Terms and Privacy Copy

BAD: `I agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy` (checkbox, required)
GOOD: `By creating an account, you agree to our Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.` (below the button, no checkbox needed in most jurisdictions — consult legal)

BAD: Long legal paragraph under the button.
GOOD: One short sentence. Link the key documents.

## 6. Trust Signals

Trust signals reduce the perceived risk of signing up or paying. Place them near the CTA or payment form.

### Near Signup CTA

GOOD examples:
- `No credit card required`
- `Cancel anytime`
- `Free for 14 days`
- `Your data is encrypted with AES-256`
- `SOC 2 certified`
- `Trusted by 50,000+ teams`

### Near Payment Form

GOOD examples:
- Lock icon + `Secured with 256-bit SSL encryption`
- `You won't be charged until your trial ends on [date]`
- `Cancel before [date] to pay nothing`
- Payment logos (Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, Stripe)
- `Questions? Chat with us` (live support signal)

BAD (absent trust signals):
A payment form with no security indicators, no explanation of billing, and no support link.

## 7. Urgency Without Manipulation

Genuine urgency converts. False urgency destroys trust the moment users notice it.

### Genuine Urgency (use these)

- Time-limited sale with a real end date: `50% off ends March 31`
- Scarcity that is real: `Only 3 spots left in the beta`
- Seasonal deadlines: `File before April 15 — connect your accountant today`
- Trial countdown: `Your trial ends in 3 days. Upgrade to keep your projects.`

### Fake Urgency (never do these)

BAD: `Only 2 left in stock!` (on a digital product)
BAD: `Offer expires in 10:00` (countdown timer that resets on every visit)
BAD: `47 people are viewing this right now`
BAD: `Limited time offer` (with no defined end date)
BAD: `Price goes up tomorrow` (said every day)

Users who spot false urgency do not just ignore it — they leave and tell others.

## 8. Feature vs. Benefit Framing

Features describe what the product does. Benefits describe what the user gets. Benefits convert; features inform.

**The formula: Feature → Benefit → User outcome**

| Feature | Benefit | User Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Automated reporting | Saves manual work | Get your Monday morning report without working Sunday night |
| 99.9% uptime SLA | Reliability | Your app is always on — no surprises for your customers |
| Role-based access | Control who sees what | Keep sensitive data away from the wrong eyes |
| Real-time sync | Always current | Everyone on your team sees the same version, always |
| Offline mode | Works without internet | Keep working on the plane or in the field |

### Rewriting Feature Copy as Benefit Copy

BAD: `Advanced filtering capabilities`
GOOD: `Find exactly what you need in seconds, no matter how much data you have`

BAD: `Multi-currency support`
GOOD: `Invoice clients in their local currency — no awkward conversion math`

BAD: `Bulk actions`
GOOD: `Update 500 records at once instead of one at a time`

BAD: `Customizable dashboards`
GOOD: `Build the view you actually want — see only what matters to you`

BAD: `API-first architecture`
GOOD: `Connect to any tool in your stack — if it has an API, it works with us`

## 9. Upgrade Prompts (In-App)

In-app upgrade prompts appear when a user hits a paywall or feature gate. They must be specific, value-focused, and non-intrusive.

### Hitting a Feature Gate

BAD: `This feature requires a Pro account. Upgrade now.`

GOOD:
```
Custom reports are available on Pro

Build reports tailored to your metrics — not just the defaults. See exactly what matters to your team.

[Upgrade to Pro — $12/month]   [See what's included]
```

### Hitting a Usage Limit

BAD: `You've reached your project limit. Upgrade to add more.`

GOOD:
```
You've used all 5 free projects

Upgrade to Pro for unlimited projects, plus version history and priority support.

[Upgrade for $12/month]   [Archive a project to free up a slot]
```

**Always offer a non-upgrade path** — like archiving or deleting. Users who feel trapped convert less.

---

## Quick Reference: Conversion Copy Checklist

- [ ] Headline leads with user outcome, not product feature
- [ ] Headline is specific (numbers, named outcomes)
- [ ] CTA uses verb + value format
- [ ] CTA supporting micro-copy addresses top objection (free trial, no card, cancel anytime)
- [ ] Social proof is specific (named, numbered, result-based)
- [ ] Social proof is placed near the CTA
- [ ] Pricing plan names signal who each plan is for
- [ ] Feature lists are written as user-facing benefits
- [ ] Urgency is real, with a verifiable end date or constraint
- [ ] Signup form is 3 fields or fewer (email, password, terms)
- [ ] Trust signals appear near signup CTA and payment form
- [ ] In-app upgrade prompts offer a non-upgrade alternative path
