# NIST AI 100-2 — Adversarial ML Taxonomy

> "No comprehensive defense against all adversarial ML attack categories currently exists."
> — NIST AI 100-2 (2024), key finding

## Core Concept

A **rigorous taxonomy of adversarial machine learning attacks and mitigations** published by NIST. Unlike OWASP LLM Top 10 (curated list) or MITRE ATLAS (TTP catalog), NIST AI 100-2 provides an **academic-grade taxonomic treatment** with precise terminology, attack class definitions, and defense strategies. It is the most rigorous reference for AI threat modeling research.

## Origin

**Apostol Vassilev et al.**, *Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations*, **NIST AI 100-2**, January 2024.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-2

## The Four Attack Classes

### 1. Evasion Attacks (Test-Time)

Crafted inputs that cause misclassification *without modifying the model*.

**Sub-classes:**
- White-box (full model access)
- Black-box (query-only access)
- Transferability (adversarial examples generated for one model work on others)
- Physical-world (adversarial patches, 3D-printed objects, makeup, glasses)

### 2. Poisoning Attacks (Training-Time)

Corrupting training data to degrade performance or introduce backdoors.

**Sub-classes:**
- Availability poisoning (degrade overall accuracy)
- Targeted poisoning (misclassify specific inputs)
- Backdoor poisoning (trigger-based misclassification)
- Clean-label poisoning (no label manipulation required)

### 3. Privacy Attacks

Extracting sensitive information from a model.

**Sub-classes:**
- **Membership Inference** — determining if a record was in training data
- **Model Inversion** — reconstructing training inputs from model outputs
- **Data Extraction** — recovering verbatim training data (esp. large language models)
- **Model Extraction** — replicating functional model via queries

### 4. Abuse Attacks

Misuse of legitimate model functionality for harmful purposes.

**Sub-classes:**
- Content generation abuse (disinformation, harassment, CSAM)
- Autonomous agent abuse (LLM-driven social engineering)
- Tool misuse in agentic systems

## Mitigation Taxonomy

NIST AI 100-2 also taxonomizes defenses:

- **Adversarial training** — robustness to evasion via augmented training data
- **Differential privacy** — provable privacy guarantees at training
- **Certified defenses** — mathematical guarantees against specific attack classes
- **Input preprocessing** — detection and filtering of adversarial inputs
- **Output validation** — constraining model outputs post-inference
- **Monitoring and anomaly detection** — runtime behavioral analysis
- **Model watermarking** — provenance tracking against theft

## Key Finding — The Comprehensive Defense Gap

NIST explicitly states: **no comprehensive defense against all attack categories currently exists**. Specific mitigations reduce specific attack classes, but trade-offs (robustness vs accuracy, privacy vs utility) mean **risk management, not risk elimination**, is the realistic posture.

**Implication for threat modeling**: AI systems must explicitly document **residual risk acceptance** — mitigations are partial, and management must consciously accept remaining exposure.

## When to Use

- **Deep technical threat modeling** for ML systems
- **Academic / research** contexts requiring precise terminology
- **Regulatory documentation** (EU AI Act high-risk AI, sector regulations)
- **Vendor assessment** — ask "which AI 100-2 attack classes do you defend against?"
- **Red team methodology** — comprehensive attack catalog
- **Security standards work** — taxonomy for emerging standards

## Strengths

- **Academic rigor** — precise, unambiguous terminology
- **Comprehensive coverage** — four attack classes covering the ML lifecycle
- **Mitigation paired** with each attack class
- **Honest about limitations** — explicit acknowledgment of defense gaps
- **NIST pedigree** — authoritative reference
- **Free and open**

## Limitations

- **Dense and technical** — requires ML background to consume fully
- **Large** — not a quick-reference list like OWASP LLM Top 10
- **Rapidly evolving field** — 2024 snapshot; updates lag novel attacks
- **Less operational** than MITRE ATLAS — more taxonomy, less TTP
- **Abuse attacks underdeveloped** — rapidly emerging area

## Relation to Other Frameworks

- **MITRE ATLAS** — operational TTP catalog; AI 100-2 is taxonomic
- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM** — curated subset; AI 100-2 is comprehensive
- **NIST AI RMF (AI 100-1)** — governance layer; AI 100-2 is technical layer within MEASURE function
- **ISO/IEC 27090** (emerging) — international standard on ML security; aligns with AI 100-2 taxonomy
- **EU AI Act** — Annex IV technical documentation requirements align with AI 100-2 categories
